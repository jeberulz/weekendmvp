## Who This Is For

You shipped.

People are paying — or at least trying the product — and now your Slack, Twitter DMs, and personal Gmail are a support desk you never asked for.

You want to be responsive without letting support own your calendar.

This is the solo-founder support system: **deflect → batch async → escalate**. Not a 24/7 chatbot theater.

If you are still pre-product, build something supportable first. Grab a narrow wedge from [startup ideas](/startup-ideas) so you are not inventing FAQs for a shapeless platform.

## The Rule: Support Does Not Own Your Calendar

You do.

Support is a scheduled job, like shipping. If every ping interrupts deep work, you will neither ship nor reply well.

Pick **two windows** a day. Example: 10:00 and 16:00. Outside those windows, you are offline for tickets unless production is down.

Customers respect consistent response more than instant chaos.

## SLA Targets by Stage

These are practical solo-founder targets, not enterprise contracts:

| MRR stage | First reply target |
| --- | --- |
| Under ~$5k | Under 24 hours |
| ~$5k–$25k | Under 12 hours |
| Above ~$25k | Under 4 hours (or hire help) |

Publish the expectation ("I reply within one business day") on your contact page. Set expectations once; stop apologizing for being human.

## Tier 1: Deflection (Docs That Actually Answer)

Most tickets are repeats.

Write four pages before you buy a fancy help desk:

1. Getting started (path to magic moment)
2. Billing / cancel / refund
3. Common errors (copy the exact error text)
4. What the product does **not** do

Put links in empty states, error toasts, and the welcome email. Deflection is not rude — it is how you protect reply quality for hard questions.

A searchable docs site beats a Notion dump nobody can find.

## Tier 2: One Inbox, Batched

Collapse channels.

One shared inbox — Help Scout, Crisp, Front, or even a dedicated support@ with templates. Not Twitter DMs + Intercom + personal email simultaneously.

Batch twice daily. Most replies are templates:

- How do I…? → link to doc + one clarifying question
- Bug → ask for URL, browser, screenshot, repro steps
- Refund → clear policy, no drama
- Feature request → thank + ask willingness to pay more if shipped

Templates should resolve roughly **80%** of volume. Customize the first sentence so it does not sound like a bot.

## Tier 3: Escalation (When Async Typing Loses)

Escalate when:

- Outage / data loss
- Billing dispute with heat
- Bug affecting multiple customers
- Explanation that would take fifteen minutes to type

For complex how-tos, record a **2–3 minute Loom** instead of a novel. Async video preserves your batching model — they watch when ready; you record once.

Live calls are rare and scheduled. Do not offer "hop on Zoom anytime" unless you are selling a high-touch plan that prices that labor in.

## Tooling by Stage

**Under $1k MRR:** one email alias, a public FAQ, Loom free tier.

**$1k–$10k:** a real shared inbox with canned replies + tagging + a tiny docs site.

**Above that:** consider a part-time support contractor with clear macros and escalation rules — before you burn out and start resenting customers.

You do not need five tools. You need one inbox you actually open.

## Make the Product Reduce Tickets

Support volume is a product signal.

Tag tickets for two weeks. The top three themes become:

- copy fixes
- empty-state improvements
- onboarding changes
- or a hard "won't do" in the docs

If the same question hits ten times, the UI failed — not the customer.

Tight products from [startup ideas](/startup-ideas) create fewer ambiguous tickets than sprawling "platforms." Scope is a support strategy.

## Refunds and Boundaries

Write the refund rule before you need it.

Example: "14-day refund if it is not a fit. After that, cancel anytime for the next cycle."

Then follow it. Arguing for forty minutes to save $29 is how founders lose evenings.

For abusive users: one clear warning, then close the account. Your nervous system is part of the business.

## A Weekly Support Cadence

**Daily:** two inbox batches; outages break the rule.

**Weekly:** 30 minutes tagging themes; ship one deflection improvement.

**Monthly:** review SLA hit rate; decide if volume justifies help.

That is enough until support is a department — which, for micro-SaaS, it may never need to be.

## FAQ

### Should I use AI auto-replies?

Maybe for triage later. Early on, wrong answers create more tickets. Templates + docs beat hallucinated policies.

### What about Discord as support?

Communities are great for peer help and dangerous as your only SLA channel. If you use Discord, still route billing/account issues to the inbox.

### How fast is too slow?

Slower than the SLA you published. Faster than that is optional. Consistency beats heroics.

## TL;DR

One inbox. Two batches a day. Docs first. Templates for the common cases. Loom for the messy ones. Publish an SLA. Let support inform the product — without owning your calendar.

Build something narrow enough to support, then grow. If you need the next product idea that will not explode your inbox on day one, start with [startup ideas](/startup-ideas).
