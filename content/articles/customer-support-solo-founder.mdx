## Can You Answer "Yes" To Any of These?

Every customer message pings your phone and wrecks your focus for the rest of the morning.

You answer the same three questions five times a week and resent it.

You haven't shipped a feature in two weeks because support ate your calendar.

You're terrified that going async will make you look unprofessional — but going synchronous is making you miserable.

If any of that sounds familiar, you're not bad at support. You're running support without a system. Solo founder customer support only works when you treat it like a product feature: designed, documented, and bounded — not something you react to all day.

Before you optimize support, make sure you're building something people actually need. [Browse ideas with clear buyer pain](/startup-ideas) and you'll get fewer "what does this do?" tickets from day one.

## Why "Always Available" Support Kills Solo Founders

The myth: great founders answer instantly. The reality: instant support trains customers to expect a one-person company to behave like a 50-person one.

Founders under roughly $5k MRR typically spend **3 to 5 hours per week on support** when organized — and **10 to 20 hours** when they're not, according to patterns across Indie Hackers and micro-SaaS accounts. Some solo operators report **23 hours weekly** on customer communication because they never batch, never document, and never say no to synchronous requests.

That's not support. That's your product development budget leaking out through your inbox.

The goal isn't to ignore customers. It's to help them well inside **two hours a week** — so the other hours go toward building the thing they paid for.

## What You Actually Need

No Zendesk enterprise plan. No 24/7 chat widget on day one. Just:

- **One shared inbox** — support@yourdomain.com forwarded to a single place you check twice daily.
- **A public response-time promise** — posted on your site so expectations are set before anyone emails.
- **A help doc with 8–12 articles** — covering the questions you already answer three times a week.
- **Four canned response templates** — research suggests roughly **80% of early tickets** fit a small set of patterns.
- **A rule for when to escalate** — refunds, outages, and angry cancelations get same-day attention; everything else waits for batch time.

That's the whole stack under $5k MRR. Add tools only when pain justifies them.

## The 2-Hour-a-Week System

### Layer 1: Deflect with docs (30 minutes to set up, saves hours forever)

Every ticket you prevent is a ticket you don't have to answer.

Start with the five questions you already know by heart:

- How do I reset my password?
- How do I cancel or change my plan?
- How do I connect [integration]?
- What counts toward my usage limit?
- How do I export my data?

Write one short article per question. Plain language. Screenshots if helpful. Link to these docs from your signup email, your settings page, and your email signature.

**Target:** 8–12 articles covering onboarding, billing, and your top three confusions. Expand when the same question hits three times.

### Layer 2: Async batches (the core of the system)

This is where the two hours live.

**Schedule two blocks:** morning (30–45 minutes) and afternoon (30–45 minutes). Open the inbox only during these blocks. Close it everywhere else — phone notifications off.

During each block: triage in 5 minutes, send canned responses for how-to and billing, handle bugs and churn risk personally, log feature requests without building today.

**Response-time targets by stage:**

| MRR band | First-reply target | What customers expect |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Under $5k | Within 24 business hours | Honest, human, not instant |
| $5k–$25k | Same business day | Reliable more than fast |
| $25k+ | Under 4 hours for urgent issues | Consider part-time help |

Under $5k MRR, **replying within 24 business hours is reasonable** — and most customers prefer a thoughtful reply tomorrow over a rushed one in four minutes.

### Layer 3: Canned responses that don't sound robotic

Four templates cover most volume. Customize the bracketed parts.

**How-to:** "Hey [name] — here's the path: [2–3 steps]. Full walkthrough: [doc link]. If that doesn't match, reply with a screenshot."

**Bug:** "Thanks for flagging this — looking into it now. I'll follow up by [time] with a fix or workaround."

**Feature request:** "Not built yet, but logged. What would you use it for day to day?"

**Billing/cancel:** "Manage your plan here: [link]. If you're canceling because something broke, tell me what went wrong — I'd rather fix it."

### Chat vs email: when to use which

**Default to email** — it's async, searchable, and doesn't invite live-chat pressure. Add chat only when your product has real-time collaboration, you're losing trials mid-onboarding, or how-to tickets exceed 15/week. Skip chat pre-revenue or if you can't staff it during business hours.

### Tools that fit solo founders

| Stage | Tool | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| Pre-revenue | Gmail + Notion docs | Free, fast, good enough |
| $500–$3k MRR | Help Scout or Plain | Email-native, calm UI |
| Want chat later | Crisp | Chat + inbox without Zendesk complexity |

Pick one inbox. Forward everything to it — don't split across Twitter DMs and Discord.

## A Real Example: How This Plays Out

Say you're at $800 MRR with 28 customers on a client-reporting tool. You get 8–12 messages a week.

You write six help articles and link them in the welcome email. Two daily batches at 9 AM and 4 PM, 35 minutes each. Four of ten tickets this week are "how do I connect Sheets?" — answered in 90 seconds with Template 1. One broken PDF export gets personal attention. Total time: about 90 minutes, not twelve micro-interruptions killing your build days.

## When Support Time Creeps Past 2 Hours

Watch for these thresholds:

- **15+ hours per week** at under $5k MRR — your docs are too thin or your product is too confusing.
- **30+ tickets per week for a month** — time for better deflection or part-time help.
- **Same bug reported 3+ times** — stop answering tickets and fix the bug.

If two or more are true, don't hire a full-time support person. Fix the product, expand docs, or add a part-time VA with your templates and decision rules documented.

## Quick Questions

### Won't customers hate waiting 24 hours?

No. They hate being ignored. A clear promise ("we reply within one business day") plus a reply that actually solves the problem beats instant auto-responses that go nowhere.

### Should I offer phone or Zoom support?

Not by default. Reserve live calls for high-value customers, enterprise deals, or debugging sessions that genuinely need screensharing. Put a Calendly link in your docs for "book a 15-minute walkthrough" — don't publish your phone number.

### When do I hire help?

When support consistently exceeds 10 hours per week despite good docs, or when first-reply time slips past your stated target for a full month. First hire is almost always a part-time VA, not a support manager.

### Is AI worth using for replies?

For drafting routine replies after you've written 50+ responses by hand — maybe. Before that, you're automating before you understand your customers. Write the templates yourself first.

## TL;DR

- **Solo founder customer support should take about 2 hours a week** under $5k MRR — two batched inbox sessions, not constant availability.
- **Deflect first with 8–12 help docs** covering onboarding, billing, and your top three confusions.
- **Four canned templates handle roughly 80% of tickets** — customize, don't sound robotic.
- **Default to email; add chat only** when real-time usage demands it and you can staff it.
- **Pick a simple inbox tool** (Gmail, Help Scout, Plain) — skip enterprise stacks until volume forces an upgrade.
