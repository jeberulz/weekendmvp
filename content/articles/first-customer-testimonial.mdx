## Who This Is For

You have three users who said "this is great" in Slack.

Your landing page still says "trusted by forward-thinking teams."

You are scared to ask because it feels needy. It is not needy. It is how B2B buyers decide. Shoutjar's 2026 roundup cites **92% of B2B buyers** as more likely to purchase after a trusted review. Foundry CRO's 2026 social-proof benchmarks put a **single text testimonial at +14% conversion** in a controlled test (the internet's recycled **+34% for three quotes** is the looser industry figure — use 14% if you want the conservative number).

You cannot A/B test your way out of an empty proof slot. You need one named sentence with a number in it.

If you do not have users yet, get ten the ugly way — [first customers with no audience](/articles/how-to-get-your-first-10-customers) — then come back. Proof without a product is fiction. Start from [startup ideas](/startup-ideas) if you still need something to put in front of people.

## Why Blank-Box Asks Fail

"Would you write a testimonial?" is a homework assignment.

People freeze. They write "Great product, highly recommend." That quote converts like a stock photo.

Ask **after a win**, in the same thread where they already praised you:

- They exported the thing.
- They sent it to a client.
- They renewed.
- They said "you saved me two hours" in chat.

That last one is already the testimonial. Your job is permission and a name, not poetry.

## The Four-Question Form (Use This)

Do not offer a blank textarea. Send four questions. Copy/paste:

1. What were you doing before this?
2. What specifically broke or ate time?
3. What changed — a number if you have one (hours, revenue, no-shows, lesson-plan time)?
4. What would you tell a friend in the same job?

You are building **before → problem → after → recommend**. Vague adjectives are a rounding error. "Cut Sunday planning from 90 minutes to 15" is the asset.

A 2025 analysis cited on Vibe Coder (1,800 SaaS landing pages) found pages with **named reviewers and specific outcomes** converted **38% better** than pages with initials and mush. Quality beats a wall of quotes.

## Where to Put It (This Matters More Than Collecting)

Foundry CRO cites a UserEvidence test: moving the **same** testimonial from below the fold to **above the fold** lifted conversion **63%** (3.91% → 6.38%).

So:

- Next to the primary CTA on the landing page.
- On the pricing page, beside the plan you want them to pick.
- In the Stripe checkout sidebar if you can without clutter.
- **Not** only on `/testimonials` that nobody visits.

A logo strip without a sentence is the lazy version. Foundry's stack: generic logos **+8%**; a named-customer claim **+22%**. You probably do not have Fortune-50 logos. You have "Maya, piano studio, 22 students." That is enough.

## Scripts That Are Not Cringe

**Right after the win (DM or email)**

> You said this cut recap time after lessons. Can I use that sentence on the site with your first name and studio type? I'll send the exact line for edits. No is a fine answer.

**If they already wrote the praise**

> Pulling this into a quote — ok to attribute as "Jordan, independent teacher"? I'll drop anything you want private.

**If they go quiet**

One bump, three days later, then stop. Two follow-ups is a relationship. Five is a collection agency.

Never pay for a *positive* review. Paying for **time** (extra month, longer trial) in exchange for an *honest* write-up is a gray area — disclose it or skip it. For v1, unpaid and specific beats incentivized sludge.

## Mine What You Already Have

Before you send a campaign:

- Support emails that say thanks.
- Product Hunt / Reddit comments (linkable, public).
- Call recordings where they quantified a result (ask before quoting).
- Screenshots of a delivered artifact they were excited about (crop PII).

Public comments are often stronger than a polished paragraph you ghostwrote. Buyers can smell ghostwriting.

## What a v1 Proof Stack Looks Like

By customer #10 you want:

1. Three named quotes with a number or a job-to-be-done.
2. One 45-second Loom (phone is fine). Video vs text gets cited in the **+25% to +80%** range depending on whose 2026 roundup you read — even a bad phone video beats "revolutionary platform."
3. Permission on file (email is enough).

Skip the animated "Sarah from NYC just signed up" widget until you have volume. Fake activity is how you train people not to trust you.

## FAQ

### Can I use a first name only?

Yes. "Priya, instructional designer" beats a full legal name they did not agree to publish. Never invent a last name.

### What if they want to stay anonymous?

Use role + situation: "Director at a 40-person training company." Weaker than a name, stronger than nothing. Do not fake a photo.

### Should I put testimonials in ads?

Only with written permission that covers ads. Site usage is not Meta usage.
