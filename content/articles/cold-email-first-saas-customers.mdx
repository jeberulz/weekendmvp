## Who This Is For

You shipped something. Nobody knows.

You don't have a Twitter audience, a newsletter, or a Product Hunt following.

You're willing to write awkward emails to strangers who have the problem you solve.

Cold email still works in 2026. Average performance got worse. **Targeted founder-led outbound** still books meetings — if you treat it like a system, not a spam cannon.

If you don't have a product yet, pause. Pick a wedge from [startup ideas](/startup-ideas), ship, then come back. Outbound without an offer is just practice typing.

## The Numbers (So You Don't Quit on Day 3)

Platform-wide B2B reply rates in 2026 sit around **~2–3.5%**. "Good" founder campaigns clear **5%+**. Elite, tightly scoped lists still hit **8–12%**.

Useful calibration from founder/YC-style funnel math (Aaron Epstein's classic shape, updated for lower reply rates):

- ~800 sends
- ~40 replies (at ~5%)
- ~10 demos
- ~1 paying customer

That's a **floor for untuned** outreach — not your ceiling. Smaller lists win: campaigns under ~50 contacts often see reply rates near **5.8%** vs ~**2.1%** on 1,000+ blasts (Belkins / Woodpecker-style 2026 analyses).

Follow-ups matter: roughly **40%+ of replies** come after the first email. One-and-done is how you donate half your pipeline.

Also: open rates are polluted by Apple Mail Privacy Protection. Optimize for **replies** and **positive replies**, not opens.

## Step 1: Define One ICP Sentence

Bad: "SaaS companies that need better marketing."

Good: "US bootstrapped B2B SaaS founders, 2–15 people, posting weekly about content or SEO pain, charging under $200/mo."

One pain. One persona. One geography if it helps list quality.

Founder-to-founder usually outperforms founder-to-VP. Small companies (1–10 people) reply more — and reply more positively — than enterprise buyers.

Still fuzzing the ICP? Steal a niche from [startup ideas](/startup-ideas) and write the email as if that buyer is real.

## Step 2: Build a Tiny List (50, Not 5,000)

For your first week:

1. Manual research: LinkedIn, niche directories, "who's hiring" pages, Indie Hackers, relevant subreddits
2. Find **work emails** (hunter tools or public pages)
3. **Verify** before send — bounce rate over ~3% wrecks domains
4. Cap the first campaign at **30–50** people you could personally explain

If you can't write one custom line about each person, your list is too big.

## Step 3: Infrastructure Without Getting Banned

Do **not** cold email from your personal Gmail day one at volume.

Minimum viable setup while you're tiny:

- A sending domain (or subdomain) used only for outbound
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC set correctly
- Warm gradually — start at ~10–20 sends/mailbox/day, not 200
- One clear unsubscribe path and a human From name

Fancy sequencer optional until a manual template works. YC-style advice still holds: write dozens personally until something converts, *then* automate.

## Step 4: The Email (Under 80 Words)

Structure:

1. **Specific observation** only true of them
2. **One-line relevance** — why you're emailing
3. **Proof or concrete artifact** — Loom, screenshot, metric
4. **Soft ask** — 15 minutes, or "worth a look?"

Template you can steal:

```text
Hi FIRST_NAME — saw COMPANY SPECIFIC_THING.

I built PRODUCT for EXACT_PAIN after hitting it myself. Short loom of how it'd work for COMPANY: LINK.

Worth a 15-min chat this week, or should I close the loop?
```

Rules:

- No "I hope this email finds you well"
- No five-paragraph origin story
- No "synergies"
- One CTA

Advanced personalization (funding round, recent post, hiring signal) roughly **doubles** reply rates vs first-name-only templates in 2026 datasets.

## Step 5: Sequence (4 Touches Max)

| Day | Touch | Job |
|-----|-------|-----|
| 0 | Email 1 | Observation + artifact + soft ask |
| 3–4 | Email 2 | New proof (customer quote, before/after) |
| 7–9 | Email 3 | Different angle or constraint ("only useful if you do X") |
| 12–14 | Breakup | "Should I close your file?" |

Space 3–7 days. Add value each time — don't "just bumping this."

## Step 6: Convert Replies Like a Human

When someone says yes:

- Reply within **2 hours** if you can
- Offer two concrete time slots or a calendar link
- Show up having used their product / read their site
- Ask about their current workaround before pitching features

Positive-reply rate is the metric that matters. Raw "interesting, send info" vanity replies don't pay rent.

## FAQ

### Is cold email dead in 2026?

Dead for lazy AI blasts. Alive for narrow ICP + real personalization + clean infrastructure. The gap between average (~3%) and good (5–10%+) is the whole game.

### How many emails until my first customer?

Budget hundreds, not dozens, if you're starting from zero brand. Improve list quality before you buy more send volume.

### Should I use ChatGPT to write the emails?

Use it to edit for brevity. Don't let it invent fake compliments. Recipients can smell generic AI. Your observation line must be human.

## TL;DR

50 verified contacts. Short founder-to-founder emails. Four-touch sequence. Optimize for positive replies. Expect ~800:1 send-to-customer until your wedge is sharp — then the math gets kinder.

No product to offer yet? Fix that before you warm a domain. Start with a concrete build on [startup ideas](/startup-ideas).
