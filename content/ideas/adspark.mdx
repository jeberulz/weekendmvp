## The Problem

A two-person marketing team at a DTC skincare brand gets a Friday-afternoon brief: launch a promo by Monday. What follows is the grind everyone in the seat knows by heart. Hunt for stock photos that do not look like stock photos. Resize the winner into six aspect ratios for Meta, Google, and TikTok. Rewrite the headline four times because the first one ran long on mobile. Export, re-export, and realize the 1080x1080 got saved over the 1080x1920. By the time the campaign is live, nobody has energy left for the only question that matters: is this the idea that actually converts?

The friction is not a lack of tools. It is that every existing tool assumes a designer with hours to spend. Small teams do not have a designer with hours to spend. They have a founder wearing the marketing hat, a generalist juggling email and paid social, or a freelancer billing three clients at once. For them, ad production is a tax paid before the real work of testing and iterating can even begin. The pain is acute and it repeats every single campaign.

The signal is loud in the places these people gather. Facebook's "Small Business Digital Marketing" group runs 70,000 members deep, "Marketing Agencies Network" holds 60,000, and "AI Marketing Innovators" 45,000, with the recurring thread being the same: ad creation is too slow and too expensive. On Reddit, r/Entrepreneur (1.1M members) and r/SmallBusiness (850K) surface the parallel complaint of teams that cannot afford an agency retainer but have outgrown DIY. The willingness to pay is already there. What is missing is a tool built for the person who needs a finished, correctly-sized ad set in under a minute, not a canvas that hands them another blank artboard.

## The Solution

AdSpark turns a product link or a two-line brief plus a short brand-tone quiz into a full ad set: correctly-sized visuals, punchy copy, and A/B variations, generated in under a minute. Paste a Shopify URL and the platform scrapes the product image, title, and price. Answer six quick questions about tone, audience, and offer. Then it fans out: hero visuals rendered natively at every required aspect ratio, three to five headline and body-copy variants scored for length and clarity, and paired A/B options so the team leaves with something to test, not just something to admire. The brand-tone quiz is the wedge. It captures voice once and applies it to every generation after, so the output reads like the brand instead of like generic AI.

**How it works:**

1. **Drop a link or brief** — Paste a product URL (the app scrapes image, title, price via Open Graph tags) or type a two-line brief; a six-question brand-tone quiz captures voice, audience, and offer
2. **Generate the set** — An image model renders on-brand visuals; a language model writes headline, primary text, and CTA variants scored for platform character limits
3. **Auto-size and vary** — Every visual is composed natively at Meta, Google, TikTok, and Story dimensions; the system pairs A/B variants that change one variable at a time
4. **Export or push** — Download a zipped, correctly-named ad set, or connect a Meta Ads account to push drafts straight into Ads Manager

You are not selling a design tool. You are selling the elimination of the production tax, so a small team spends its hours on the idea instead of the export.

You are routing existing demand, not creating it.

## Competitive Landscape

Ad-creative software is crowded at the top and thin at the bottom. Enterprise platforms own agencies and large brands; generative toy tools own hobbyists. The correctly-sized, brand-aware, sub-minute product for the two-person marketing team is the gap:

- **AdCreative.ai** — The closest direct competitor and the category's marketing juggernaut. Generates ad creatives and copy at scale with conversion-score overlays. Outputs can skew generic and the credit model burns fast once a team gets serious. Its pricing anchors to mid-market, which leaves the true entry tier underserved. Starter around $29/mo, scaling through roughly $149/mo Professional to $599/mo Ultimate.
- **Canva** — The default for small teams that hate dedicated tools. Enormous template library, Magic Design AI, real resize features. But it is a general design canvas, not an ad engine: it will not scrape a product link, will not score copy against platform limits, and still hands you a blank artboard. Free / Pro around $15/mo / Teams around $10/user/mo.
- **Smartly.io** — Advanced creative automation and dynamic optimization built for performance marketers running serious paid-social volume. Powerful feedback loops, deep Meta and TikTok integrations. Priced and onboarded for funded teams, typically starting in the low thousands per month with minimum spend commitments. Enterprise-only, no self-serve entry.
- **Celtra** — Enterprise creative automation and digital asset management for large brands and publishers. Best-in-class versioning and brand-safety controls, custom-quote contracts, complex onboarding. Explicitly not built for a solo marketer who needs an ad set before lunch. Custom enterprise pricing, typically five figures annually.
- **DIY: ChatGPT plus Canva plus a spreadsheet** — What most small teams actually do today. Free-ish and infinitely flexible, but every step is manual: prompt, copy, paste, resize, rename, repeat. The stitching is the tax AdSpark removes. Effectively $0, paid in hours and context-switching.

**Your Opportunity**

No incumbent will move down to an opinionated, sub-$30 SMB tool. Smartly.io and Celtra would cannibalize enterprise deals; AdCreative.ai's credit economics push users up, not down; Canva will not narrow its general canvas into an ad-specific engine. Win on three things they will not chase: (1) link-or-brief-to-finished-set in under a minute with zero blank artboard, (2) a brand-tone quiz that makes output sound like the brand instead of like AI, and (3) native multi-platform sizing plus paired A/B variants shipped by default, so the team leaves with something to test.

## Business Model

Subscription SaaS with a free tier that converts on generation volume, not locked features. The path to $1M ARR runs through roughly 2,100 paid users blended across Starter and Pro, or about 12,000 free users converting at a 15 percent rate. Per-generation cost (image model plus language model) lands around $0.05 to $0.20 for a full ad set, so even the entry tier holds a healthy margin.

- **Free** ($0) — 3 ad sets per month, watermark-free, single brand profile, all export sizes — the lead-gen wedge
- **Starter** ($29/mo) — 50 ad sets per month, unlimited brand profiles, A/B variant pairing, CSV copy export
- **Pro** ($79/mo) — Unlimited ad sets, Meta Ads Manager push, performance-scored copy, priority generation, 3 seats
- **Agency** ($199/mo) — Everything in Pro plus 10 seats, client workspaces, white-label export, and shared brand libraries

An annual prepay (two months free) removes friction for teams that hate recurring charges, and a usage-metered overage on Free-tier users who blow past 3 sets nudges the upgrade at the exact moment of need.

**Unit Economics**

- **$0.05–0.20** — Cost per generated ad set
- **~85%** — Gross margin (Starter and Pro blended)
- **$40–70** — Target CAC
- **~$430** — LTV (14-mo blended)

## Recommended Tech Stack

Optimize for brief-in to ad-set-out in under a minute. The hard part is not the model calls; it is reliable product scraping, deterministic multi-size composition, and keeping copy inside every platform's character limits.

- **Next.js 14 plus Vercel** — App Router for the dashboard, serverless functions for the generation pipeline, Vercel Cron for scheduled report emails. One repo, minimal infra overhead.
- **Supabase (Auth plus Postgres plus Storage)** — Tables: users, brand_profiles (tone, palette, audience answers), generations (brief, size, copy_variants, image_url), and assets in Storage. Row-level security keyed on user id.
- **Replicate or fal.ai for image generation** — Hosted SDXL or Flux endpoints for on-brand visuals with brand-color conditioning. Swap models without re-architecting; render each aspect ratio as its own composited layer.
- **Claude plus GPT-4o for copy** — Claude for primary headline and body generation with prompt caching on the brand-tone profile (a real margin win); GPT-4o as failover. A strict JSON schema returns scored variants with character counts per platform.
- **satori plus resvg for composition** — Render text-over-image ad layouts to PNG server-side at exact platform dimensions, so sizing is deterministic and never depends on a browser canvas.
- **Stripe Billing plus Meta Marketing API** — Free / Starter / Pro / Agency tiers with metered overage; the Meta Marketing API pushes generated creatives as drafts into Ads Manager for the Pro tier.

## AI Prompts to Build This

Copy and paste these into Claude, Cursor, or your favorite AI tool.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of "AdSpark": paste a product link or a two-line brief, answer six brand-tone questions, and get a correctly sized ad set with copy variants.

Stack: Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Storage, Auth with email magic link), Claude for the copy, Replicate for the base image, satori and resvg for the text-over-image layouts. Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on, each user reads only their own rows):
- brand_profiles(id, user_id, name, tone, palette jsonb, audience, offer)
- generations(id, user_id, brand_profile_id, brief, source_url, sizes jsonb, copy_variants jsonb, created_at)
- assets(id, generation_id, size, storage_path)
Storage: one bucket named ad-assets.

Screens: /login, /brand (the six-question quiz), /generate.
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, REPLICATE_API_TOKEN.

Do not build: billing, plans or monthly caps, pushing ads to Meta, a second copy model, annual options, an admin area.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the three tables and the ad-assets bucket exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves AdSpark: a link or brief in, a full ad set out.
1. POST /api/generate takes { brand_profile_id, source_url?, brief? }. If source_url is present, fetch the page and read og:image, og:title and product:price:amount to seed the brief.
2. Load the brand profile and build one system prompt from its tone, palette, audience and offer.
3. Ask Claude for 4 copy variants as strict JSON: { headline, primary_text, cta, char_counts: { meta, google, tiktok } }. Reject any variant over a platform limit.
4. Ask Replicate for one base hero image in the brand palette.
5. For each size (1080x1080, 1080x1920, 1200x628, 1080x1350), compose the winning headline over the image with satori and resvg, and save the PNG to Storage.
6. Pair the copy variants into A/B sets that change exactly one thing. Return signed URLs for every asset.
Empty state: with no brand profile, send the user to the six-question quiz.
Done when: pasting a product URL returns four correctly sized images and four copy variants within about a minute.
```

**3. Landing Page**

```text
Build a one-page landing site for AdSpark, an AI ad-creative generator for small marketing teams.
Hero: "From product link to full ad set in under a minute." Sub: "Paste a link, answer six questions about your brand, and get correctly sized visuals, scroll-stopping copy and A/B variations ready to launch." One button: Join the waitlist.
Sections: a before and after that turns a raw product photo into a 4-size ad set as you scroll, the problem (small teams pay a production tax before they can test ideas), how it works in four steps (drop a link, generate the set, auto-size and vary, export), and an FAQ on brand-voice accuracy, supported ad platforms and export formats.
Waitlist: store the email in a waitlist table in Supabase. No other service.
Style: Geist, deep aubergine #4c1d3f against off-white, generous whitespace.
Done when: the page renders on a phone and a submitted email appears in the waitlist table.
```

**4. Branding Package**

```text
Use a design or image tool for this one. A coding agent cannot draw a logo.
Brand for AdSpark: a wordmark and a compact icon suggesting a spark igniting a frame.
Colors: deep aubergine #4c1d3f, one warm-gold accent and two neutral greys. Type: Geist for the interface and headings, Geist Mono for metrics and character counts.
Voice rules: lead with speed, always show the finished set and not the canvas, and promise testable copy, never perfect copy.
Deliverables: wordmark, icon, a 6-icon set (link, brief, brand tone, generate, resize, A/B), and a one-page brand sheet with three sample ad headlines in the brand voice.
Done when: each deliverable is saved in one folder and the icon reads at 32 px.
```
