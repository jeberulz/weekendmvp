## The Problem

Knowledge workers spend more than two hours every day inside email — reading, triaging, drafting, deferring, and re-reading threads they meant to answer yesterday. The inbox is an infinite to-do list owned by everyone else. A founder opens Gmail at 7 a.m.: 47 unread. Fourteen are newsletters, nine are FYI cc's, six are genuine asks with buried deadlines, three are angry customer threads needing careful replies today. The rest is scheduling ping-pong and "quick question" messages that each cost fifteen minutes of context-switching.

The mental load is decision fatigue. Every message asks: Is this urgent? Can I ignore it? Reply now or batch for Friday? Most people develop brittle hacks — color labels, filters that miss edge cases, starred items that become a second inbox, draft replies that sit open for days because the right tone takes energy they do not have at 4 p.m.

Existing tools help at the margins. Filters sort. Snooze hides until Monday. Smart folders surface "important" senders via opaque heuristics. None actually *do the work*: prioritize with context, draft in your voice, schedule the send, and surface only what needs human judgment. Result: inbox anxiety, slow replies on messages that matter, and a guilt loop around inbox zero that never closes. A consultant billing $150/hour who loses ninety minutes to triage burns $225 before lunch. Email management software is a multi-billion-dollar category because this problem scaled with async work and AI-generated outreach.

## The Solution

Inbox Zero Agent (working name: InboxZen) is an AI inbox agent on top of Gmail and Outlook — a chief of staff for your mail. It scores priority from sender history, deadline language, and thread context; drafts replies in your voice; schedules sends; and surfaces a daily attention queue of five to twelve threads that actually need you.

Not another email client. Users keep Gmail or Outlook. A Chrome extension adds a side panel; the agent uses official APIs so UI changes do not break it. Wedge: founders, consultants, account managers, and recruiters who pay $15/month to reclaim an hour without learning Superhuman-style shortcuts.

**How it works:**

1. **Connect Gmail or Outlook** — OAuth through the extension; the agent indexes recent sent mail to learn your tone, sign-off patterns, and typical response length.
2. **Morning attention queue** — Each day opens with a ranked list: reply-now, reply-today, delegate, archive. Newsletters and automated noise never appear unless you ask.
3. **One-click draft replies** — Tap any thread; Claude generates a reply in your voice with context from the thread and your sent-mail style guide. Edit, approve, or discard.
4. **Schedule sends** — Batch non-urgent replies for 9 a.m. Tuesday when you are fresh; the agent queues them through the provider's send API.
5. **Weekly inbox report** — See where time went: top senders, average response lag, threads you snoozed three times (a signal to unsubscribe or template).

Retention compounds: approved drafts train voice; "not urgent" dismissals sharpen scoring. After two weeks the agent knows your biggest client always ranks high and vendor cold outreach can auto-archive.

## Competitive Landscape

The category splits into premium clients, filter-and-snooze utilities, AI writing add-ons, and free platform bundles. None combine priority intelligence, voice-matched drafting, and scheduled sending in a single lightweight agent that works inside the inbox users already have.

- **Superhuman** — The premium email client benchmark. Blazing UI, split inbox, AI snippets, team read statuses. Requires leaving Gmail's native UI and learning new shortcuts. **$30/month** individual; team pricing higher. Strong brand, high switching cost, but overkill for users who want assistance without migrating clients.
- **SaneBox** — AI-powered filtering, snooze, attachment sorting, and follow-up reminders. Works with existing clients via IMAP. Excellent at hiding noise but does not draft replies or learn voice. **~$7/month** (Snack plan) to **$36/month** (Dinner) depending on features and mailboxes.
- **Fyxer AI** — Meeting notes and email drafting assistant oriented toward executives. Integrates with Gmail and Outlook; focuses on meeting follow-ups and inbox cleanup. Pricing typically **$10–15/user/month** range for AI features. Closer competitor on drafting, but less emphasis on daily priority queue and scheduled batch sending.
- **Mailbutler / Mixmax** — Gmail and Outlook add-ons for templates, tracking, scheduling, and smart assistant features. Mailbutler Smart Assistant from roughly **$5/month**; Mixmax tiers from **$13–33/user/month**. Broad feature sets; AI is one module among many, not a unified agent experience.
- **Gmail Smart Features (bundled)** — Summarize thread, smart reply chips, priority inbox heuristics. **Free** with Google Workspace. Good enough for casual users; no voice learning, no cross-thread priority scoring, no scheduled batch workflow, and Outlook users get nothing.

Position as: *Keep Gmail. ## Business Model

Per-user SaaS with a generous trial that demonstrates the first morning queue and three draft replies. Individual tier is the PLG engine; team tier adds shared analytics, SLA tracking, and admin controls for agencies and sales pods.

- **Free Trial** (14 days) — Full attention queue and drafting on one mailbox; capped at 50 AI drafts to prevent abuse.
- **Individual** ($15/month) — Unlimited drafts, priority scoring, send scheduling, weekly reports, one Gmail or Outlook account.
- **Team** ($40/user/month) — Everything in Individual plus shared team dashboard, response-time SLAs, shared templates, admin voice guide, priority rules per role (sales vs. support).
- **Annual discount** — 2 months free on yearly billing to improve cash flow and reduce churn.

**Unit Economics (illustrative)**

- **$0.08–0.25** — LLM cost per draft (Claude Haiku for triage, Sonnet for final reply), amortized over daily batch scoring
- **~80%** — Gross margin at Individual tier with sensible caching of sender profiles and thread summaries
- **$25–60** — Target CAC via productivity content and Chrome Web Store discovery
- **110%+** — Net revenue retention as solo users invite teammates for SLA visibility

MRR path: 500 Individual = $7.5K/mo; add 50 ten-seat teams = $27.5K/mo total. Voice and priority history reduce churn — retraining elsewhere hurts.

## Recommended Tech Stack

The build is a Chrome extension for UI and provider API access, a Next.js backend for auth and AI orchestration, and Postgres for user data and learned preferences. Gmail API and Microsoft Graph cover mail read, draft create, and scheduled send. Avoid scraping DOM — API-first keeps the product stable across UI changes.

- **Chrome Extension (Manifest V3)** — Side panel for attention queue, thread preview, draft editor, and approve/schedule actions. Background service worker syncs new mail on interval and push notifications where supported.
- **Next.js App Router + Vercel** — Landing page, OAuth callback routes, billing portal, weekly report emails, and API routes for extension communication. Server Actions for draft generation jobs.
- **Gmail API + Microsoft Graph** — Read threads, list messages, create drafts, send or schedule send (Gmail: users.messages.send with scheduled time via draft + Apps Script alternative or provider-specific scheduling; Outlook: deferred send via Graph). Store refresh tokens encrypted.
- **Claude (Anthropic API)** — Haiku for priority classification and thread summarization; Sonnet for voice-matched draft generation. System prompt includes user style guide extracted from last 200 sent messages.
- **Postgres (Supabase or Neon)** — Tables: users, mail_accounts, threads, priority_scores, drafts, send_queue, style_profiles, feedback_events. pgvector optional for semantic sender clustering.
- **Stripe Billing** — Individual and per-seat Team subscriptions; usage metering optional if draft volume exceeds fair-use thresholds.

Weekend MVP: Gmail only, queue + draft + manual approve. Week two: Outlook and scheduled send.

Copy and paste these into Claude, Cursor, or your favorite AI tool.

**1. Chrome Extension + Gmail OAuth Shell**

```text
Build a Chrome extension (Manifest V3) plus Next.js 14 App Router backend for an AI inbox agent called Inbox Zero Agent.

Extension:
- Side panel UI showing "Attention Queue" (ranked thread list with priority badge, subject, sender, one-line summary)
- Background service worker that polls backend every 5 minutes for queue updates
- Content script minimal — no DOM scraping; all data via backend API
- OAuth flow: extension opens popup to /api/auth/gmail on the Next.js app; store session token in chrome.storage.local

Backend routes:
- GET /api/auth/gmail — start Google OAuth with scopes: gmail.readonly, gmail.compose, gmail.send
- GET /api/auth/gmail/callback — exchange code, encrypt refresh token, store in mail_accounts table
- GET /api/queue — return today's attention queue for authenticated user
- POST /api/draft — given thread_id, generate and return draft reply

Postgres schema:
- users(id, email, plan, stripe_customer_id, created_at)
- mail_accounts(id, user_id, provider ENUM[gmail,outlook], refresh_token_encrypted, last_sync_at)
- threads(id, mail_account_id, provider_thread_id, subject, sender, last_message_at, priority_score, summary, status ENUM[pending,drafted,scheduled,done,archived])
- drafts(id, thread_id, body, status ENUM[generated,approved,sent,scheduled], scheduled_for, created_at)
- style_profiles(id, user_id, tone_notes, avg_length, sign_off, example_snippets JSONB)

Landing page headline: "Your inbox, minus the triage." Subhead: AI priority queue and voice-matched drafts for Gmail.
Use TypeScript throughout. Environment variables for Google client ID/secret and Anthropic API key.
```

**2. Priority Scoring + Attention Queue**

```text
Implement the daily attention queue pipeline for Inbox Zero Agent.

Sync job (runs every 15 min per connected account):
1. Gmail API: list threads with label INBOX, max 100, ordered by internalDate desc.
2. Skip threads already scored in the last 6 hours unless new messages arrived.
3. For each new/changed thread, fetch snippet + last 3 messages (plain text, strip quoted replies).

Priority classifier (Claude Haiku, JSON mode):
Input: thread subject, sender, snippet, user role (optional), historical sender_stats (avg response time, past priority labels).
Output schema:
{
  priority: "reply_now" | "reply_today" | "delegate" | "archive",
  score: 0-100,
  reason: "one sentence",
  detected_deadline: "ISO date or null",
  summary: "one sentence thread summary"
}

Scoring rules in system prompt:
- reply_now: explicit urgency, angry customer, deadline within 24h, VIP sender marked high in style profile
- reply_today: direct question, meeting request, client thread without urgency markers
- delegate: FYI with action item for someone else, internal routing
- archive: newsletter, automated notification, cold outreach, no-reply needed

Store results in threads table. Attention queue API returns top 15 threads where priority in (reply_now, reply_today) sorted by score desc.

Feedback loop: when user marks "not urgent" or archives from queue, log feedback_events and downgrade sender priors.
```

**3. Voice-Matched Draft Replies + Scheduled Send**

```text
Build draft generation and scheduled sending for Inbox Zero Agent.

Style profile bootstrap (on first connect):
- Fetch last 200 sent messages via Gmail API.
- Claude analysis: extract tone (formal/casual), typical length, common sign-offs, greeting patterns, 5 example reply pairs (incoming → sent).
- Store in style_profiles.

Draft generation (POST /api/draft):
Input: thread_id, optional user_notes ("softer tone", "push meeting to next week").
Fetch full thread. Build prompt:

System: "You write email replies matching this user's voice exactly."
Include style_profiles fields and 3 example snippets.
User: thread transcript + user_notes.
Output: plain-text reply body only, no subject line, no markdown.

Return draft for side-panel editor. User can edit inline.

On approve:
- Gmail API users.drafts.create with threadId binding.
- If send_immediately: users.messages.send with draft id.
- If scheduled_for timestamp: store in send_queue table; cron job (Vercel Cron every 5 min) checks due items, sends via API, marks thread done.

Track: drafts generated, approval rate, edits before send (measure voice accuracy). Gate unlimited drafts behind Individual plan; trial capped at 50.
```
