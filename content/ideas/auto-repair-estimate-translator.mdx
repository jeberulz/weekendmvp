## The Problem

The customer does not distrust the shop because the labor rate is high. They distrust the estimate because it is a parts cannon in a language they do not speak. "R&R compressor, evac/recharge, orifice tube, dye test" is a $1,800 decision they will Google into a YouTube nightmare. r/MechanicAdvice (1.5 million), r/cars (3.3 million), and r/AutoRepair (182k) are that nightmare, live.

Shops already paid for the diagnosis. Bosch, Autel, and Hitachi sell AI-assisted hardware in the thousands. AutoLeap, Shopmonkey, Tekmetric, Mitchell, ShopWare run the RO. RepairPal sits on the consumer side as a price benchmark — the thing a nervous customer opens while you are still under the hood. Nobody owns the five minutes after you hand them the tablet: plain language, annotated photos, "what happens if we wait."

The positioning claim you will hear is "40–60% higher approval." Treat that as a sales line, not a published RCT. The real metric is time-to-yes and comeback rate on declined work.

## The Solution

A customer-facing explainer that sits on top of the estimate the shop already wrote. Paste the RO (or pull it from Shopmonkey / Tekmetric). Get a one-pager: what failed, why it failed, what we will do, what it costs in English, three annotated photos, and a "defer this / do this now" split. SMS the link. The advisor still talks. They stop translating.

SaaS from $50/month in the Ideabrowser ladder; the street price that matches independent shops is $99–$299/month. Hardware vendors will not unbundle their tablet UI to do this.

**How it works:**

1. **Ingest the RO** — Advisor pastes line items or connects the shop OS. VIN + mileage + concern are required so the explainer is not generic.
2. **Translate + evidence** — Model rewrites each line into cause / fix / risk-if-wait. Advisor drops 2–4 bay photos; the app labels the failed part on the image.
3. **Send + decide** — Customer gets an SMS link. Approve / defer per line. Deferred work becomes a 60-day reminder, not a lost RO.

## Market Research

Auto repair and maintenance: $744.4 billion in 2025 to $1,056.6 billion by 2034 (3.97% CAGR, IMARC / Mordor-class cuts). AI automotive repair services: $684 million in 2023, $797.54 million in 2024 (16.75%), $2.72 billion by 2032 (SkyQuest). Broader automotive AI: $48.59 billion by 2034. Cox Automotive's 2026 "fragmented reality" note: 76% of dealerships are increasing AI budgets — and independents still run on paper and Shopmonkey.

This is not a TAM for another scanner. The scanner market is a graveyard of bundles. The TAM is "shops that already pay $200–$600/month for an OS and still lose the approval conversation." Facebook's Auto Repair Professionals group (45k) is the channel.

ADAS and EV work makes the English problem worse. "Calibrate forward camera after windshield" is a $400–$1,200 line the customer will defer because it sounds optional. The explainer that says "without this, the car may not brake" is the product. Do not claim a 40–60% approval lift on the landing page. Show one redacted before/after RO.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Bosch / Autel / Hitachi** — AI diagnostics inside hardware that costs thousands. Dealership-oriented. The customer never sees the output.
- **AutoLeap AIR** — shop OS with AI features. Subscription unspecified in the Ideabrowser cut. They will add an explainer if this wedge prints. Integrate, do not pretend you replace the OS.
- **Shopmonkey / Tekmetric / Mitchell / ShopWare** — the RO systems shops already pay. Integration targets. None of them made the customer-facing one-pager the product.
- **RepairPal** — consumer price benchmark. The competitor in the customer's other tab. You will not beat their database. You can beat "here is *this* shop's photo of *this* compressor."

**Your Opportunity**

Wedge is trust, not diagnosis. Free 5-code / 5-line translator (shop email) → $50 Basic → $250 Pro (photos, SMS, defer reminders) → $100 add-ons (multi-location, Spanish) → $1,000+ enterprise for a 15-bay group. Do not sell to the 200-store platform in year one. Sell to the 4-bay independent who already lost a $2,400 job to "I need to think about it."

Unit economics:

- LLM + SMS per estimate is under $0.20
- $99/month at 40 estimates is about $2.50/RO — less than the advisor's five minutes
- Churn is "we forgot to send the link," so the SMS step is mandatory on Pro

## Recommended Tech Stack

- **App:** Next.js 15 on Vercel
- **Auth / billing:** Clerk + Stripe
- **DB:** Supabase — `shops`, `estimates`, `line_items`, `photos`, `decisions`
- **LLM:** Claude for the rewrite (parts language is messy; it handles "R&R" better than a small model)
- **Images:** Upload to R2; SAM or a cheap vision call for the label box
- **SMS:** Twilio
- **Later:** Shopmonkey / Tekmetric API — v1 is paste

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of the "Repair Estimate Translator": a service advisor pastes the repair order they already wrote, and the customer gets a plain-English page with what failed, why, what it costs, annotated photos and a choice to approve or defer each line. The shop's prices are never changed.

Stack: Next.js (App Router, TypeScript), Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Storage, Auth with email magic link for shop staff), the Anthropic API for the rewrite, Twilio for the text that carries the link. The customer page needs no login and opens by an unguessable link. Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on, a shop reads only its own rows; the customer page reads through a server route that checks the token):
- profiles(user_id, shop_name, phone)
- estimates(id, user_id, token, vin, mileage, concern, customer_phone, status, created_at, sent_at, expires_at)
- line_items(id, estimate_id, raw_line, price_cents, cause, fix, risk_if_wait, priority, safety_flag boolean, decision, decided_at)  priority is do_now or can_wait; decision is approved, deferred or pending
- photos(id, estimate_id, storage_key, label, x_pct, y_pct)
- followups(id, estimate_id, line_item_id, due_on, status)

Screens: /login, /estimates/new (paste the lines and their prices), /estimates/[id] (review, photos and send), /e/[token] (the customer page), /bay (decisions on a tablet).
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID, TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN, TWILIO_PHONE_NUMBER.

Do not build: billing, shop plans, integrations with Shopmonkey or Tekmetric, diagnostic hardware, automatic reminder texts to customers, a vision model.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the five tables exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves the Translator: the customer understands each line, the prices are exactly the shop's and the shop sees every decision.
1. The advisor pastes the repair order one line per row with its price, plus the VIN, the mileage and the customer's concern. Store the price in cents from what the advisor typed. A price is never produced or changed by the model.
2. Send the lines, the VIN, the mileage and the concern to the Anthropic API with a strict JSON schema { items: [{ line_index, cause, fix, risk_if_wait, priority: "do_now" | "can_wait" }] }. Each cause and fix is plain English in one or two sentences. Check that there is exactly one item per line and that no item adds work or parts that are not on the line.
3. Set safety_flag in code for lines that match a fixed list of safety systems (brakes, tires, steering, suspension, airbags, lights). Only flagged lines may say a delay is a safety risk; strip that wording from any other line.
4. The advisor uploads 2 to 4 photos and places a label on the failed part by tapping the picture. Store the label and its position as percentages. Show the label drawn on the photo.
5. Send the customer a text with the link through Twilio, to the number the customer gave the shop, from a number that says who it is. Honor STOP. The page at /e/[token] opens without a login, shows each line with its price, the explanation and the photos, and has Approve and Defer buttons per line and a total of what was approved.
6. The bay view lists decisions as they arrive. A deferred line creates a followup due in 60 days for the shop to act on; do not text the customer automatically. Show approval rate per advisor to the owner.
Rules: links expire after 30 days. The page says the advisor is the person to ask. Never show a price the shop did not enter.
Empty state: with no estimates, show the paste form and a sample repair order.
Done when: a sample order produces one explanation per line with the shop's exact prices, a non-safety line never says safety, the customer link works without a login and expires, an approval shows on the bay view and a deferred line creates a 60-day followup.
```
