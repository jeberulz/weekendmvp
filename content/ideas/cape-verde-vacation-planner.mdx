## The Problem

Cape Verde has quietly become one of Europe's fastest-growing winter-sun destinations, and the people booking it are almost all first-timers. A UK family sees a £299 easyJet fare to Sal, books it on impulse, and then hits a wall: which of the ten islands should we actually visit? Can we day-trip to Boa Vista? Is the ferry reliable? When is the water calm enough to swim? The excitement of "we're going to Cape Verde" collapses into thirty browser tabs, a Facebook group full of contradictory advice, and a nagging fear of wasting the one week they get.

The core difficulty is that Cape Verde is not one place — it is an archipelago where each island has a completely different character, and the transport between them is genuinely hard to plan. Sal is flat, beachy, and built for resorts. Boa Vista is emptier, with unspoilt dunes. Santiago holds the cultural and historic heart. But the two most popular islands, Sal and Boa Vista, are only linked by flights in summer and by a notoriously unreliable ferry the rest of the year. Domestic flight timetables are published only a few months out and can generally be bought only on the carrier's own website, and the inter-island boats are old, uncomfortable, and prone to breaking down. This is the single biggest organisational headache in the entire trip, and it is exactly the part generic planning tools get wrong.

So travellers fall back on a patchwork: a Lonely Planet article for context, a weather site to guess the best month, a blog for "Sal or Boa Vista," a forum thread for ferry gossip, and a booking site for hotels — none of them talking to each other, none of them producing an actual day-by-day plan. The pain is not a lack of information. It is that the information is scattered, often out of date, and never assembled into a decision. People do not want ten open tabs. They want someone to say: for your dates, your budget, and the fact that you have never been, here is the island to pick and here is what each day looks like.

## The Solution

Cape Verde Vacation Planner is an AI trip planner that does only one destination — Cape Verde — and does it properly. Instead of a blank global chatbot, it asks a handful of targeted questions (travel dates, who's going, budget band, and what you actually care about: beaches, diving, hiking, turtles, music, or culture) and returns a complete, day-by-day itinerary grounded in the real logistics of the islands. Because it is destination-specialised, it can encode the things generic planners hallucinate: which islands connect and when, ferry versus domestic flight, the November-to-June sweet spot when the sea is calmer and flights are more reliable, and the honest truth that a Sal-to-Boa-Vista "day trip" is usually a bad idea.

The output is not a wall of text — it is a decision. The planner tells you which island (or realistic two-island combination) fits your dates, lays out each day with morning and afternoon suggestions matched to your interests, flags the best time to book each leg, and drops in bookable flights, hotels, and tours inline via affiliate links. Every recommendation is filtered through a curated Cape Verde knowledge base rather than the open web, so the ferry advice is current and the island logic is right. You land on a shareable itinerary page you can send to whoever you're travelling with.

**How it works:**

1. **Answer six questions** — Dates, party size, budget band, and your top interests (beaches, hiking, diving, culture). Takes under a minute, no signup required to see a plan.
2. **Get your island match** — The planner recommends the right island or a realistic multi-island route based on your dates and the real inter-island transport calendar, not wishful thinking.
3. **Receive a day-by-day itinerary** — Each day is filled with morning and afternoon activities matched to your interests, with the best time to go and practical transport notes baked in.
4. **Book inline and share** — Flights, hotels, and tours appear as bookable links inside the plan; you get a shareable itinerary URL to send to your travel companions.

## Market Research

Cape Verde is in a genuine boom, and the demand is concentrated in exactly the first-timer segment this product serves.

- **Cape Verde received a record 981,354 tourists in 2024, an 11.8% jump on 2023** and a 39.1% rise on 2022 as the sector cleared its post-pandemic recovery, according to the national statistics office (INE). This is a market growing double-digits year over year.
- **2025 set another record at roughly 1.2 million visitors**, with the average length of stay rising to 4.8 nights and national hotel occupancy climbing from 60% in 2024 to 72% in 2025 — more people, staying longer, filling more rooms.
- **The United Kingdom is the single largest source market at 36.3% of arrivals**, ahead of Germany (11.5%) and the Benelux countries (10.1%) — an English-speaking, high-intent audience that plans and books online.
- **Demand is lopsided by island: Sal accounts for 59% of overnight stays and Boa Vista 36.6%**, meaning nearly the entire market lands on just two islands and needs the exact "which island / can I hop" guidance this tool provides.
- **New direct UK routes launched in 2025** — easyJet added London Gatwick to Sal (three times weekly from March 2025, return fares advertised under £300) and TUI opened East Midlands to Boa Vista — expanding capacity and pouring fresh, under-informed first-timers into the funnel.
- **Travel is one of the most durable affiliate niches**, with the global travel industry projected to top $17 trillion by 2035 and the average travel website reportedly earning north of $13,000 per month — a large, evergreen monetisation surface for a focused niche site.

## Competitive Landscape

The market splits into generic AI planners that know a little about everywhere and traditional resources that assemble nothing. Neither owns "Cape Verde" as a specialised, current, decision-making product.

- **Layla** — The strongest general-purpose AI planner for building a bookable itinerary with live prices, priced around $49/year for its premium tier. Excellent breadth, but it treats Cape Verde as one more destination and does not encode the ferry-versus-flight reality or the seasonal island connections.
- **Wanderlog** — Popular trip organiser with a Pro tier around $40/year. Great for maps and collaborative lists once you already know your plan, but it is a container for decisions, not a source of Cape Verde-specific ones.
- **Vacay** — Subscription AI travel assistant at roughly $9.99/month. Conversational and capable, but the same generalist gap applies: no curated Cape Verde transport knowledge, so island-hop advice is unreliable.
- **Mindtrip and Wonderplan** — Both offer free core planning (Mindtrip added in-chat flight booking in 2026). Fast and frictionless, but their output for a niche archipelago is a generic skeleton that misses exactly the logistics that make or break a Cape Verde trip.
- **Traditional resources (Lonely Planet, tour operators like Cape Verde Experience, Facebook groups, blogs)** — Accurate context and human advice, but static or slow. A blog cannot produce your dated, personalised, bookable day-by-day plan, and a tour operator locks you into a package.

**Your Opportunity**

Win by being the deepest, most current authority on one destination instead of the shallowest on all of them. A Cape Verde-only planner can encode the real inter-island calendar, keep the ferry advice fresh, and rank for the entire "Cape Verde vacation," "Cape Verde itinerary," "Sal or Boa Vista," and "things to do in Cape Verde" keyword cluster — an SEO moat the horizontal players will never bother to build for a single archipelago. When a first-timer searches, the specialist that produces an actual plan beats the generalist that produces a skeleton.

## Business Model

Affiliate-first, with a thin premium layer. The itinerary is free because the plan is the funnel: every recommended flight, hotel, and tour is a monetised, high-intent booking moment at the exact instant someone has decided to go.

- **Free itinerary** ($0) — Full day-by-day plan, island match, and shareable link. Monetised entirely by inline affiliate links (hotels, flights, tours). This is the SEO and word-of-mouth engine.
- **Traveller Pass** ($19 one-time) — A downloadable, offline PDF of the itinerary plus a printable Cape Verde essentials pack (transport cheat-sheet, packing list, Creole phrase card, emergency numbers). Sells to the "I just want it sorted" holidaymaker.
- **Planner Pro** ($9/month) — For repeat and multi-island travellers: unlimited saved itineraries, live flight-price alerts on your dates, and priority re-planning when a ferry or flight schedule changes.

Affiliate is the core engine. Booking.com's programme pays affiliates 25%–40% of its own commission (Booking.com typically earns around 15% from the property) and reports a seven-day EPC north of $160, while flights and tours route through networks like Travelpayouts, Viator, and GetYourGuide. With Cape Verde's average 4.8-night stay, a single converted hotel booking on a mid-range property can return more than a Traveller Pass sale — and it repeats across flights and a tour or two per itinerary.

**Unit Economics (illustrative)**

- **~$0.02** — LLM cost to generate one full itinerary (gpt-4o-mini with a cached knowledge base)
- **$15–45** — Blended affiliate revenue per converted booking (hotel + flight + tour mix)
- **3–6%** — Realistic booking-conversion rate on a high-intent, ready-to-travel visitor
- **$0** — Marginal cost of the free plan beyond pennies of inference, so organic SEO traffic drops almost straight to margin

## Recommended Tech Stack

The product is small: a questionnaire, a retrieval-grounded generation step, and an itinerary renderer with affiliate links. The hard part is not the AI — it is keeping the Cape Verde knowledge base current so the transport advice stays right.

- **Next.js on Vercel** — App Router for a fast, SEO-first site; server components so every itinerary and island guide is crawlable and ranks. Static island/keyword landing pages plus the dynamic planner in one repo.
- **Supabase (Postgres + Auth)** — Store itineraries, saved trips, and the curated Cape Verde knowledge base (islands, activities, seasonal transport calendar). pgvector for retrieval so generation is grounded in your data, not the open web.
- **OpenAI gpt-4o-mini (or Claude Haiku)** — Cheap, fast itinerary generation with the knowledge base injected as context and prompt caching on the static island facts to keep per-plan cost near two cents.
- **Travelpayouts + Booking.com affiliate + Viator/GetYourGuide** — Affiliate rails for flights, hotels, and tours. Travelpayouts aggregates several travel programmes behind one integration so you are not wiring five APIs by hand.
- **A lightweight CMS or MDX for the knowledge base** — Keep island facts, activity data, and the ferry/flight seasonal calendar in editable content so a non-engineer can update transport advice when schedules shift.
