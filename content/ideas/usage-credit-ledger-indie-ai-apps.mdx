## The Problem

**Concept proposal. Buyer demand and pricing remain to be validated.**

An indie AI app offering prepaid usage needs to explain more than a balance. A customer may want to see what a request reserved, what it ultimately consumed and what was returned when the request failed. The proposed product is a small operational ledger that makes those transitions visible.

The target buyer is a founder maintaining usage accounting in application code. The working hypothesis is that scattered balance updates make support questions and failure handling difficult to reconstruct. A repeated callback or interrupted job should not create an unexplained extra debit.

The customer-facing job is straightforward: connect a usage request to a readable account history. The founder-facing job is to trace that same history without manually editing a balance or guessing which part of a failed request completed.

## The Solution

Build a ledger workspace with a small integration contract for reserving, settling and releasing product credits. Begin with a simulator and an account timeline. Separate the app's usage units from real money so a prototype cannot be mistaken for a payment processor.

**How it works:**

1. **Define a usage unit.** Choose the product's credit meaning and display it consistently.
2. **Reserve a request allowance.** Record a stable request key before work begins.
3. **Settle the completed work.** Post the final usage and release any unused reservation.
4. **Release failed work.** Record the failure outcome without silently deleting earlier entries.
5. **Explain the history.** Show a readable timeline tied to the underlying request.

Each transition should be repeatable without creating an additional posting. Corrections should appear as new entries with a reason and reference, so the operator can explain what changed. The account view should distinguish available credits from credits temporarily reserved for running work.

The weekend MVP is a demonstration of this lifecycle using fictional accounts and request outcomes. Production payment events, tax treatment, migration of customer balances and provider-specific charging rules require separate work after the accounting contract is reviewed.

## Market Research

[Stripe documents billing credits](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/billing-credits), while [Metronome](https://metronome.com/) and [OpenMeter](https://openmeter.io/) describe usage-oriented billing and metering products. These are relevant existing approaches to investigate. Their existence does not establish a separate market for a small application-credit ledger.

The key discovery question is where the founder's current system fails to explain a request lifecycle. Review a sanitized support case, an interrupted job and a repeated callback. Determine whether the problem belongs in the existing billing system, the application runtime or a dedicated operational view.

An early pilot should test whether the founder can answer a customer's balance question from the timeline and whether the integration fits the product's actual failure states. Pricing, repeat demand and the cost of supporting different usage models remain to be validated.

## Competitive Landscape

- [Stripe billing credits](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/billing-credits) are an existing billing approach to evaluate.
- [Metronome](https://metronome.com/) offers a usage-based billing platform.
- [OpenMeter](https://openmeter.io/) offers usage metering and billing tooling.

The proposed focus is the application request lifecycle and a support-readable credit history. Compare that job with the existing products before describing a gap. A buyer that already has a satisfactory billing and account explanation may need no additional ledger.

The initial differentiation to test is a narrow integration contract, clear failure transitions and a timeline understandable to both the developer and the customer. Avoid a promise of universal billing compatibility until actual integrations have been built and tested.

## Business Model

**Proposed model:** a developer software subscription with allowances for active accounts and recorded transitions. A starter offering could provide the integration contract and account view; a team offering could add investigation notes, exports and access controls. No tested price is established yet.

A guided integration pilot may be more useful initially than a self-serve checkout. Map the customer's usage unit, reservation policy and failure behavior before recommending changes to an existing balance system.

Costs to investigate include transaction volume, account history retention, concurrency, support and migration assistance. The business should be careful about scope: managing application entitlements and usage records does not automatically make it suitable for holding or transferring customer money.

A possible acquisition approach is a developer guide showing how a fictional failed AI request releases its reservation and remains understandable in the timeline. Pair it with a small simulator rather than an unsupported savings claim.

## Recommended Tech Stack

Use a relational database with transactional postings and a server-side API. Represent credits using an explicit unit and a precise numeric format. Keep reservations separate from posted entries and derive balances from the defined accounting rules.

The prototype needs stable request keys, owned accounts and a timeline that explains each transition. A simulator can generate success, failure, retry and cancellation cases without connecting a payment provider or external model.

Before a production integration, review authorization, concurrent requests, correction behavior and recovery after interruptions. A visible history is valuable only if its postings remain consistent with the product's own usage contract.
