## The Problem

Global teams lose **7+ hours a week** (Ideabrowser #2161 TimeSync market signal) to cross-timezone scheduling. A sales rep in New York trying to schedule a prospect in Sydney. A design team coordinating a cross-functional review across EMEA, APAC, and the Americas. A founder hiring their first engineer in Europe. Current tools either show "everyone's availability" as a raw union (Calendly group polls, When2Meet) or pick a slot without considering that 6am for Singapore is a terrible time to think clearly.

Calendly dominates the scheduling-link category with **20M+ users** and a $3B+ valuation, but charges **$10–$20 per user per month**. For a 10-person team that's $1,200–$2,400/year just for booking links. SavvyCal and Cal.com chip at the edges with better UX and lower pricing, but the "AI conflict resolver that understands working hours" is not yet a standard feature. The wedge is opinionated defaults: never suggest outside a person's working hours, never suggest back-to-back meetings, always prefer slots after lunch.

The second wedge is flat-team pricing. Calendly charges per user even for a round-robin routing group where technically only one person takes the meeting. For a growing company that's pricing friction. Charging $49/mo flat up to 10 team members undercuts Calendly by 80% at team size 10. Cal.com's self-hosted angle is technical overhead that most teams don't want; managed hosted + AI features + flat pricing is the sweet spot.

## The Solution

A Calendly-class scheduling SaaS with three opinionated improvements. One: AI slot suggestion that respects working hours by timezone (never shows 6am Sydney as a "free" slot even if the calendar is empty). Two: team round-robin that distributes incoming bookings across configured teammates based on load balance (not just availability). Three: embeddable widget that drops into any website without iframe hell. Flat pricing: free solo, $19 Solo Pro, $49 Team up to 10 members.

**How it works:**

1. **Connect calendar** — Google / Outlook / iCloud. Set working hours per day.
2. **Share link** — Booker picks a slot in their own timezone. AI respects yours.
3. **Auto-confirm + Zoom** — Calendar event created both sides; Zoom/Meet link attached.

The AI conflict resolver uses a simple rule engine + LLM judgment. Rules: within working hours, not back-to-back, not 30 minutes before end-of-day. LLM: for hard cases, "suggest 3 slots where both attendees will have reasonable energy." Over time, user feedback tunes the system ("never Mondays before 10am" as learned preference). This is a genuine differentiator that the incumbents haven't shipped because they optimize for enterprise compliance, not individual ergonomics.

## Competitive Landscape

Five competitor classes: Calendly + Cal.com (category leaders), SavvyCal (premium UX), Motion + Reclaim (AI calendar defrag adjacent), native calendar group-find-time (Google/Outlook built-in), and Doodle/When2Meet (legacy polls). None combine AI slot suggestions with flat-team pricing and an embeddable widget.

- **Calendly** — Category leader. 20M+ users, $3B valuation. Deep Salesforce/HubSpot integrations. Per-user pricing scales linearly; enterprise focus. Round-robin requires Teams plan at $16/user/mo. $10 Standard → $16 Teams → $20+ Enterprise per user/mo
- **Cal.com** — Open-source + hosted. Developer darling, 30K+ GitHub stars. Flexible but requires setup. AI features nascent. Hosted pricing is per-user. Self-hosting has ops overhead most SMBs avoid. Free self-hosted; $12/user/mo Teams hosted
- **SavvyCal** — Premium UX challenger. Overlay feature (see booker's calendar + your calendar at once) is beloved. Priced higher per-user than Calendly; no flat-team tier. No AI slot suggestions yet. $12 Basic → $20 Premium / $40 Team per user/mo
- **Motion / Reclaim.ai** — AI calendar tools but focused on defragmenting existing calendars and auto-scheduling tasks. Not primarily booking-link tools. Pricing $34–$49/user/mo — overkill for teams that just want scheduling. Motion $34/user/mo; Reclaim.ai $10–$18/user/mo

**Your Opportunity**

Flat $49/mo up to 10 team members. AI slot resolver that respects working hours and energy patterns across timezones. Embeddable widget that works without iframe contortion. Tagline: "Scheduling that feels like it thought about your 8am, not just your calendar."

## Business Model

Flat pricing, not per-user. Free tier for solo indie; Solo Pro for single users who need the advanced AI; Team for small companies. LLM cost per booking is minimal (\<$0.005); infra dominated by calendar API quota. Gross margin stays above 93% at scale.

- **Free** ($0) — 1 event type, 1 calendar, 10 bookings/mo, “Powered by” footer
- **Solo Pro** ($19/mo) — Unlimited event types, multi-calendar, AI slot resolver, embeddable widget, custom branding
- **Team** ($49/mo) — Up to 10 members, round-robin routing, team calendars, Salesforce/HubSpot integrations, SSO

**Unit Economics (illustrative)**

- **~$0.60/mo** — Infra cost/tenant
- **~96%** — Gross margin Solo
- **$30–$60** — Target CAC
- **5–9%** — Free → Solo conv.

MRR path: 200 Solo + 30 Team = $5.3K/mo. 1K Solo + 150 Team = $26.4K/mo. At 4K Solo + 600 Team = $105.4K/mo ($1.26M ARR). Switching costs are high once users bake the scheduling link into email signatures, website footers, and CRM workflows.

## Recommended Tech Stack

Calendar OAuth + slot computation + AI layer. The hardest part is handling calendar API quotas and correctly computing free/busy across attendees in different timezones. Rest is SaaS plumbing.

- **Next.js 14 App Router** — Admin + booker-facing pages. `/[username]/[event-type]` public booking URLs as Server Components for fast first paint and SEO.
- **Supabase + Postgres** — Tables: `users`, `teams`, `calendars`, `event_types`, `bookings`, `routing_rules`. RLS per-user and per-team.
- **Google + Microsoft Calendar APIs** — Google Calendar API v3 + Microsoft Graph API. OAuth with calendar scopes. Use freebusy endpoints for availability computation; `events.insert` on booking. Handle token refresh per tenant.
- **Claude Haiku for slot judgment** — When multiple valid slots exist, Claude ranks them considering working hours, both-party timezones, prior bookings patterns, and user-supplied preferences ("never before 10am Mondays"). Invoked only for Pro+ tiers to preserve margin.
- **Zoom + Google Meet API** — Zoom OAuth for auto-creating meeting URLs on booking confirmation. Google Meet link is free with Google Calendar invites. Microsoft Teams via Graph API for Outlook users.
- **Stripe Billing + Widget CDN** — Stripe Checkout for Solo/Team. Widget served from Cloudflare CDN as a Shadow-DOM iframe embed; one-line `<script>` install for any website.

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves the Smart Meeting Scheduler: timezone-aware slots that are never silly, and a booking that cannot double-book.
1. Availability: fetch the host's free and busy times from Google Calendar (cache for 5 minutes). Apply the host's working hours by weekday in the host's timezone. Subtract existing confirmed bookings from the database too. Build candidate slots of the event's duration with its buffer, then apply min_notice_hours and max_advance_days.
2. Rank in plain code with named constants. Add 10 for 10:00 to 11:30 local, add 5 for 13:30 to 15:00, subtract 5 within 30 minutes of the end of working hours, subtract 15 within 30 minutes of the start, subtract 10 for a slot back to back with another meeting, subtract 20 when either person would be outside 08:00 to 19:00 in their own timezone.
3. The booking page detects the visitor's timezone from the browser, shows the top 5 slots as "Best for both of you" and the rest under "All times", in the visitor's timezone with the host's timezone beside it.
4. Booking inserts the row first. The unique key on event_type_id and start_at stops two visitors taking the same slot, and the loser sees "That time was just taken, here are the next ones". Then create the Google event with a Meet link and the visitor as an attendee so Google sends the invite, and save meeting_url and google_event_id.
5. Unit test the slot code across a daylight saving change and for a host in Sydney: a 06:00 Sydney slot is never offered even with an empty calendar.
Rules: store times in UTC and convert only for display. Never show a slot the host's calendar marks busy.
Empty state: with no event types, show a form to create the first one.
Done when: the unit tests pass, a visitor in another timezone sees sensible slots, two simultaneous bookings for one slot produce one booking and one friendly refusal, and the visitor receives a Google invite with a Meet link.
```
