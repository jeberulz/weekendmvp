## The Problem

Electing S-Corp is the easy win. The hangover is monthly. A one-person consultancy at $80K–$250K now has officer payroll, a “reasonable salary,” shareholder distributions, estimated-tax reserves, and a CPA who only wants the packet in March. The owner still pays themselves whatever is left in the checking account. Receipts sit in a shoebox. April is a forensic rebuild billed by the hour.

The supply side made it worse. The US accounting workforce shed more than 300,000 professionals between 2020 and 2023 — a 17% drop (BLS, cited in Ideabrowser verified research). US accounting degree completions fell to 55,152 in 2023–2024, a 20-year low (AICPA 2025 Trends). Firms drop the smallest clients first. That is the solo S-Corp: too little revenue per staff hour, too much paperwork.

Budget tools do not close the loop. QuickBooks Solopreneur is about $20/month and still costs 3–5 hours a week. Merritt Bookkeeping is a flat $250/month for QBO reconciliation — no payroll, no tax, no salary math; “ask your CPA.” Pilot Core is $299/month with annual prepay and a startup-shaped floor; S-Corp tax filing starts at $2,000/year. Heard does the full stack at $129–$255/month and only for therapists. Bench’s Grow plan sat at $199/month until the December 2024 shutdown left clients mid-season with a D-minus BBB aftertaste. Local CPAs start around $500/month and are booked.

This site already has a 1099 e-file wedge in [Freelancer Tax Filing Bot](/ideas/freelancer-tax-filing-bot) and a ledger wedge in [AI Bookkeeping for Freelancers](/ideas/ai-bookkeeping-for-freelancers). Neither answers the S-Corp operator’s monthly question: what can I safely pay myself, what do I reserve, what is broken. Reddit’s r/Bookkeeping (~82K), r/Accounting (~1.3M), and r/smallbusiness (threads with 300+ comments on bookkeeping cost) repeat it. Facebook’s Small Business Owners Who Need Bookkeepers (~56K) is a referral board, not a product.

## The Solution

Monthlii is a **human-reviewed monthly finance desk** for one-person S-Corps — not a CPA firm, not tax advice, not an LLM inventing your reasonable salary. AI sorts bank and card feed. A human (you, then contractors) reviews flags, runs a rules-based owner-pay and tax-reserve snapshot, and ships a one-page brief plus a CPA-ready packet. The CPA you already have stays the CPA. You are the ops layer they wish every client arrived with.

Scope v1 like a weekend product that becomes a practice. One S-Corp, US, owner-only payroll, no employees, no multi-state nexus, no K-1s from other entities. ICP: consultant, realtor, coach, or small agency at $80K–$250K who elected S-Corp for the tax savings and then discovered payroll. If they need 1120-S signed, partner with an EA/CPA or refer — do not file without a credential (see Ideabrowser extra: PTIN / EA / CPA for paid 1120-S work).

Trust is the product. Bank tokens stay in Plaid/Rutter. QBO is the ledger of record so the client is never locked into proprietary books (the Bench lesson). The model categorizes and explains; it does not invent tax positions. The brief is heuristics plus a flagged queue, not “you should take this salary” as legal advice.

**How it works:**

1. **Setup sprint** — Connect Plaid (or Rutter) and Gusto/Justworks-style payroll. Import or clean 3–6 months in QBO. Establish a starting balance sheet and a payroll baseline. One-time $497 so messy books are not a monthly objection.
2. **Books run in the background** — Daily categorization. Weekly 30-minute human pass on outliers, transfers, and owner draws. Payroll confirmation against the posted salary.
3. **Month-end brief** — One page: cash in, cash out, suggested owner pay vs distributions (rules-based), estimated-tax reserve, and one fix list. Packet exports the CPA can file from.
4. **Stay current** — Quarterly reminder cadence. Optional annual 1120-S add-on only if a licensed partner signs. Churn risk is April; the brief that still shows up in June is the renewal.

## Market Research

The category is bookkeeping. The gap is **S-Corp monthly clarity without stealing the CPA relationship**.

- **More than 5 million S-Corps operate in the US** — IRS tax-year 2021 showed 5.3 million individual returns reporting S-Corp income; the 2025 S-CAP Act record repeats the 5 million+ figure (verified research).
- **Nonemployer firms: about 29.8–30.4 million in the US, $1.7–$1.8T in receipts** (OnePC / Census-class compilations in the competitive pack). Nonemployer growth ~2.7% annually 2012–2023, faster than employer firms.
- **Solopreneur count ~29.8 million in 2026**, up from 26.5 million in 2024 (Crevio / Solo Business Hub compilations). New business applications ~440K/month, ~90% above pre-2020.
- **Accountant pipeline is broken** — 300,000+ left 2020–2023; degree completions at a 20-year low. AICPA calls it a pipeline crisis through ~2029. Optimistic 12% enrollment uptick (AACSB spring 2025) does not refill firms this year.
- **AI in small-business finance jumped from 37% (2023) to 85% (2025)** (Intuit QuickBooks Small Business Insights). Sorting is cheap; interpretation is not.
- **Search is real but not the wedge.** “Monthly bookkeeping” and “outsourced bookkeeping” carry high commercial intent; “S-Corp bookkeeping service” is thin. r/smallbusiness has ~2.5 million members. First 100 clients come from comments, not SEM.
- **Bench collapsed December 2024.** Competitor pricing jumped. Negative Bench-by-Mainstreet reviews were still landing on Capterra into 2026. That is a live outreach list.

**Stage:** mature software, emerging “finance desk” sub-niche. Heard proved niche bookkeeping can raise $26M on therapists alone. You do not need that scale. You need one profession and 20 seats at ~$400 blended.

## Competitive Landscape

Name the money. Generic “we do books” loses to Merritt on price and to a CPA on trust.

- **Merritt Bookkeeping** — Flat **$250/month**, QBO only, email support. Strength: cheap close. Weakness: no payroll, no tax, no S-Corp salary. Explicitly “talk to your CPA.”
- **Pilot** — **$99/month** AI tier or **$299/month** Core (annual prepay, startup expense floor). S-Corp/partnership tax filing **starts at $2,000/year** (live pricing, August 2026; the $750 SKU is new single-member LLCs, not S-Corps). Strength: brand, startup ICP. Weakness: not a solo realtor’s product; annual lock-in.
- **QuickBooks Live** — Assisted **$200–$400/month**, Full-Service **$500–$700/month**. Industry-agnostic. Strength: Intuit trust. Weakness: no S-Corp advisory.
- **Heard** — Therapists only, **$129–$255/month**, S-Corp in Premium, $26M funded. Strength: niche depth. Weakness: if you are not a therapist, you are not a customer.
- **Bench (historical)** — Grow **$199/month**, proprietary ledger, shutdown Dec 2024, D-minus BBB. Use as a cautionary tale, not a partner.
- **Local CPA** — **$500/month+**, personalized, tax-season shaped, capacity constrained.
- **DIY** — QBO Solopreneur **~$20/month** plus ChatGPT. 3–5 hours a week. Zero optimization.

**Your Opportunity**

No one sells a flat $300–$500 month to a one-person S-Corp outside Heard’s vertical: owner-pay snapshot, payroll confirmation, CPA-ready packet, month-to-month, existing CPA stays. Merritt stops at recs. Pilot wants startups and $2,000 tax add-ons. Heard will not take your realtor. Own “pay / reserve / fix” on one page. Price in the gap between $250 Merritt and $500 CPA. Do not compete with [Freelancer Tax Filing Bot](/ideas/freelancer-tax-filing-bot) on 1099 e-file — that is a different entity and a different last mile.

## Business Model

Service-shaped SaaS. Software is the factory; the SKU is the brief.

- **Free** — 15-minute S-Corp health check + one-page gap report (current pay vs a defensible salary range). Reddit/Facebook wedge.
- **Setup sprint** — **$497 one-time**. Clean 3–6 months, QBO migrate, payroll baseline, starting balance sheet.
- **Monthly desk** — **$300/month** simple one-stream S-Corp; **$500/month** multiple accounts or messy income. Reconciliation, payroll confirm, quarterly reminders, one-page brief. Flat by complexity, not transaction count (Pilot’s volume model punishes growth).
- **Continuity** — **$50–$150/month** extras (KPI, extra entities) later. **Annual 1120-S** **$750–$1,200** only with a licensed partner.
- **Not v1:** enterprise $10K/year, white-label for CPA firms (that is the real scale path once the packet format is trusted).

**Unit Economics (illustrative)**

- **under two hours** — Target operator time per client per month or the $300 SKU dies
- **about 20 seats** — ~$8K MRR at $400 blended; Ideabrowser napkin for ~$100K year-one with setups and tax add-ons
- **about 40 seats** — Solo ceiling ~$16K/month bookkeeping before you hire
- **$2K–$8K** — Capital to start (QBO, Plaid, insurance, a one-pager)
- **30–60 days** — Time to first paid sprint if you already have a niche

Path to about $5K MRR: ~13 desks at $400, or 10 at $500. This is a lifestyle practice with a real ceiling, not a venture-scale usage API. Say that on the landing page so you attract operators, not VCs.

## Recommended Tech Stack

QBO is the system of record. You are the review queue and the PDF.

- **App:** Next.js + TypeScript on Vercel. Client portal: login, brief archive, task list, CPA share link.
- **Ledger:** QuickBooks Online API (not a custom GL). Never trap the client in a Bench-shaped silo.
- **Banking:** Plaid or Rutter for feeds if you need a second path; prefer QBO bank feeds when they already live there.
- **Payroll:** Gusto (including Solo) or similar; confirm posted salary vs QBO wages. You do not become a payroll processor in v1.
- **AI:** Claude or GPT for categorization suggestions and brief drafts. Human merge required. No advice language in generated copy.
- **Ops:** Notion or Airtable for the first five clients; graduate to your portal when the packet template is stable.
- **Auth / payments:** Clerk + Stripe. Engagement letter + ACH for monthly.
- **Comms:** Resend for the month-end email; Slack/email for the review queue.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of "Monthlii": the operator console for a human-reviewed monthly close for one-person S-Corps. Import a month of transactions, sort them with rules the operator controls, review the exceptions and ship a one-page brief and a packet for the client's CPA. It is bookkeeping and records, not tax, legal or investment advice.

Stack: Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Auth with email magic link for the operator), the Anthropic API to suggest categories where no rule matches. Transactions come in by CSV (bank, card and a payroll register). Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on, the operator reads only their own clients):
- clients(id, operator_id, name, owner_pay_pct, tax_reserve_pct)  the two percentages are numbers the operator types, never defaults
- months(id, client_id, period, status, created_at)  status is open, review or sent
- line_items(id, month_id, posted_at, description, amount_cents, source, category, category_source, flag, changed_by, changed_at)  category_source is rule, model or operator
- category_rules(id, client_id, pattern, category)
- briefs(id, month_id, markdown, created_at)
- audit_events(id, month_id, actor, action, before jsonb, after jsonb, at)  append-only

Screens: /login, /clients, /months/[id] (the exception queue), /months/[id]/brief (the one-page brief), /months/[id]/packet.
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), ANTHROPIC_API_KEY.

Do not build: billing, setup fees or retainers, QuickBooks, Plaid or Gusto connections, a client portal, tax filing, any salary opinion field.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the six tables exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves Monthlii: a month closed by rules the operator owns, and a brief that never reads as a tax position.
1. Import a month of CSV transactions (date, description, amount, source). Save each as a line item.
2. Categorize in this order: a matching category_rules pattern first. If none matches, ask the Anthropic API for a category from the fixed list and mark the item as a suggestion with category_source model. Nothing posts as final without the operator accepting it.
3. Queue exceptions in code: transfers between accounts, owner draws, any uncategorized item over an amount the operator sets, and a payroll line that does not match the payroll register total.
4. The queue lets the operator accept, recode or add a rule from an item. Every change writes an audit_events row with who, before and after.
5. Generate the one-page brief in code: cash in, cash out, net, a suggested owner pay and distribution split using owner_pay_pct with the formula and the inputs shown, a tax reserve using tax_reserve_pct labeled "a heuristic, not a tax position", and a numbered fix list from the open exceptions.
6. Export a CPA packet: a profit and loss, the transaction CSV and the payroll register as files and a share link. Mark the month sent only when no exception is open.
Rules: put "Bookkeeping and records. Not tax, legal or investment advice." on every page and export. If a percentage is empty, the brief says to set it rather than guessing. Never imply a reasonable-salary opinion.
Empty state: with no clients, show the add form and the CSV columns.
Done when: a sample month imports and categorizes by rules, a model suggestion stays pending until accepted, the queue catches a transfer and an owner draw, the brief shows its formulas and the heuristic label, and the audit log records each change.
```
