## The Problem

Most small-business wellness programs are one-size-fits-all disasters that employees quietly ignore. HR pays for a bundle of generic yoga videos and step challenges, then watches participation crater within a quarter. The person drowning in financial stress never wanted a meditation app. The warehouse crew with back pain never needed a nutrition webinar. The new parent running on four hours of sleep skips every "lunch-and-learn." The budget gets spent, a wellness box gets checked, and turnover keeps climbing anyway.

The frustration is loud and specific in the communities where HR managers actually talk. On Reddit, r/humanresources (159K members) runs recurring threads with titles like "Office Wellness Programs — BS or Actually Worth It?", and r/employeeengagement (13K members) keeps circling back to whether personalized wellness apps can double as retention tools. r/smallbusiness (1.2M members) is full of owners asking for something scalable and affordable that does not require an enterprise contract. Facebook groups such as "Employee Wellness Trends 2025" and "Roots Corporate Wellness" (640+ engaged members of HR managers and consultants) echo the same complaint: the tools exist, but almost none of them personalize, and the ones that do are priced for companies with 5,000 seats.

The downstream cost is real money. Generic programs fail because engagement, not feature count, is what drives outcomes — and engagement collapses the moment the content stops feeling relevant to the individual. For a 40-person company paying roughly $10 to $15 per employee per month, a program nobody uses is thousands of dollars a year buying nothing but a compliance line item. Meanwhile the retention problem the program was supposed to solve keeps eating recruiting budgets, institutional knowledge, and morale. The core pain is not "we lack a wellness vendor." It is "every wellness vendor treats a 55-year-old machinist and a 26-year-old designer as the same person."

## The Solution

WellnessIQ is an AI-driven wellness platform that builds a personalized health journey for every employee instead of shipping the same content to everyone. During onboarding, each person completes a short assessment covering goals, schedule, and preferences; the platform combines that with anonymized engagement patterns and optional wearable data to recommend the right mix of mental health, fitness, nutrition, sleep, and financial-wellness modules. Someone flagged as financially stressed gets debt-management workshops and emergency-savings nudges. Someone in a physically demanding role gets mobility and recovery routines. The recommendations adapt continuously: the more an employee uses it, the sharper the personalization becomes.

Managers get the other half of the product — a dashboard that finally answers "is this working?" with real engagement and outcome data by program, so wellness stops being a faith-based expense. The wedge is personalization that actually works, delivered at a price and setup speed a small business can say yes to, with HIPAA- and GDPR-grade privacy baked in so employees trust it enough to participate.

**How it works:**

1. **Assess** — Each employee completes a two-minute intake on goals, schedule, and health interests; the company connects its HR system in a few clicks so rosters and departments sync automatically.
2. **Personalize** — The AI engine builds an individual wellness plan from the intake plus anonymized behavioral signals, choosing modules across mental health, fitness, nutrition, sleep, and financial wellness rather than pushing a fixed catalog.
3. **Adapt** — As people engage (or skip), the model re-ranks recommendations weekly, surfaces one-tap adjustments, and folds in optional wearable data for higher-signal nudges.
4. **Prove** — Managers see a live dashboard of engagement and outcomes by program and department, plus ROI-oriented reports they can take to ownership at renewal time.

## Market Research

The timing here is unusually clean: a large, reliably growing market where the dominant products are still semi-generic and the SMB segment is openly underserved.

- **The global workplace wellness market is projected to grow from about $56.5B in 2025 to roughly $92B by 2035, a CAGR near 5.0%** (Future Market Insights). This is not a niche — it is a mature, tens-of-billions category with a steady growth floor.
- **The corporate wellness segment specifically is valued around $64.89B in 2025 and forecast to reach $90.7B by 2032 at a 4.9% CAGR** (GlobeNewswire / Coherent Market Insights, Oct 2025). Two independent research houses landing near the same 2032 figure is a strong signal the trend line is real, not a single analyst's optimism.
- **Wellness apps as a sub-category are expected to hit about $33.78B by 2032 at a much faster 14.9% CAGR** (Precedence Research). Digital-first delivery is where the growth concentration is, which is exactly the surface this product lives on.
- Structurally, large enterprises still hold roughly 63.9% of 2024 revenue and onsite delivery about 60.5%, while SMB adoption and digital/remote delivery are the fastest-rising slices (industry segmentation data). North America leads with 40%+ revenue share; APAC is the fastest-growing region. Translation: the money today sits in enterprise and onsite, but the growth vector points straight at affordable digital tools for smaller teams.

The takeaway for a solo founder: you are not betting on whether the market exists. You are betting on capturing the underserved edge of a proven, growing category before incumbents refactor to reach it.

## Competitive Landscape

Corporate wellness is crowded at the enterprise tier and thin at the SMB tier — and almost nobody combines true AI personalization with a small-business price point.

- **Virgin Pulse (Personify Health)** — The category leader, serving an estimated 14M+ users with deep HRIS/HCM integrations, a broad content ecosystem, and a rewards engine. Powerful, but expensive for small teams and still mostly rules-based rather than deeply adaptive. Pricing runs roughly **$4 to $12 per employee per month** on negotiated contracts.
- **Limeade (acquired by WebMD in 2024)** — Strong engagement analytics, customizable communications, and API connectors to major HR platforms. Aimed at US/UK enterprises, typically 500+ employees, with little evidence of AI-first personalization. Pricing is typically **$5 to $10+ per employee per month**.
- **Wellable and Vantage Fit** — The SMB-friendly incumbents: activity/challenge platforms, health content, fast setup, mobile-first. Affordable at roughly **$2 to $7 per employee per month**, but shallow on personalization — largely rules-based modules and thin integrations.
- **MAXIOM** — A 2025 AI-native entrant with adaptive coaching chatbots, wearable integration, and real-time stress detection. Technically the closest competitor on the AI thesis, but with limited enterprise case studies and an SMB value proposition that is still evolving. Pricing is not publicly disclosed.
- **Point solutions and HRIS add-ons** — Headspace for Work and Calm for Business (mental health), Wellhub/Gympass and ClassPass Teams (fitness), plus bundled wellness modules inside Workday, ADP, and SAP SuccessFactors. Each solves one slice; none delivers cross-domain personalization tuned to the individual.

**Your Opportunity**

The gap is explicit: affordable, explainable, AI-first personalization built for the 50-to-500-employee company, with one-click HRIS onboarding and cross-domain modules (including financial wellness and shift-aware scheduling) that the enterprise players either overprice or under-deliver. Incumbents will not move down-market easily — cheap self-serve SMB pricing cannibalizes their per-seat enterprise economics and their broker-led sales motion. Win on three things they will not chase: a setup measured in clicks instead of quarters, personalization that visibly improves week over week, and pricing a 40-person shop approves without a procurement cycle.

## Business Model

Per-employee SaaS with a value ladder that turns a free diagnostic into a recurring platform contract. The free assessment seeds HR-manager trust and generates qualified leads; the paid tiers monetize on outcomes, not seat count alone.

- **AI Wellness Needs Assessment** ($0) — An interactive tool that scores a company's current wellness gaps and employee engagement. Pure lead-gen and trust-builder.
- **Personalized Wellness Starter Pack** ($49 one-time trial) — A basic AI-tailored plan for a small team, designed to convert curiosity into a first paid experience and collect feedback.
- **Wellness Platform Subscription** ($5 to $20 per employee per month) — The core recurring product: continuous personalization, all wellness modules, employee dashboards, and manager reporting.
- **Advanced Analytics + Integration Add-Ons** ($200 to $1,000 per month) — Deep analytics, cohort benchmarking, and premium HRIS/payroll integrations for companies that want to prove ROI rigorously.
- **Enterprise Licensing / White Label** ($10K to $100K+ per year) — Custom-branded deployments for larger firms, brokers, and benefit aggregators.

**Unit Economics (illustrative)**

- **$10 to $15** — Blended revenue per employee per month across SMB plans
- **~80%** — Target gross margin (cloud + AI inference are the main variable costs, kept low with cached recommendations)
- **$300 to $600** — Target CAC per company account (LinkedIn + community-led)
- **High LTV** — Per-company contracts across dozens of seats plus annual renewals push lifetime value well above single-seat SaaS

**Path to MRR:** Land 500 small businesses averaging roughly $100 in monthly platform spend and you clear about $50K MRR — the 18-month target implied by the research. At a 40-seat average and $10 to $15 per employee, a few hundred accounts is a genuine mid-five-figure monthly run rate, comfortably above bootstrap thresholds.

## Recommended Tech Stack

The hard parts are not the wellness content — they are reliable HRIS integration, defensible data privacy, and a recommendation loop that actually improves. Build for multi-tenant SaaS with health-grade security from day one.

- **Next.js 14 (App Router) + Vercel** — One repo for the marketing site, employee app, and manager dashboard; Edge routes for webhooks and Vercel Cron for weekly re-ranking and summary jobs.
- **Supabase (Postgres + Auth) with row-level security** — Tables for organizations, employees, assessments, modules, engagement events, and recommendations. RLS keyed on org and employee is non-negotiable for health data.
- **Merge.dev (unified HRIS API)** — One integration surface for BambooHR, Gusto, Workday, ADP, and more, instead of building each connector by hand. This is the single biggest onboarding accelerator.
- **OpenAI (gpt-4o-mini) + a lightweight ranking model** — gpt-4o-mini classifies free-text goals and generates plan copy; a simple scoring/ranking layer over engagement events drives the weekly personalization, with prompt caching to hold margin.
- **Terra API (optional wearables)** — Normalized access to Fitbit, Apple Health, Garmin, and Oura so wearable signals feed recommendations without per-device engineering.
- **Stripe Billing** — Per-seat metered subscriptions, the one-time starter SKU, and annual enterprise invoicing, with a self-serve customer portal.
- **Compliance layer** — HIPAA-aware hosting (BAA), GDPR/CCPA consent flows, audit logging, and clear employee opt-in — treat this as a feature and a moat, not overhead.

Copy and paste these into Claude, Cursor, or your favorite AI tool.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Create a Next.js 14 (App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind) project for "WellnessIQ," a multi-tenant employee wellness SaaS. Provision Supabase with these tables:
- organizations (id, name, plan, seat_count, created_at)
- employees (id, org_id, hris_id, department, role_type, timezone, consent_at)
- assessments (id, employee_id, goals JSONB, schedule JSONB, interests JSONB, created_at)
- modules (id, domain TEXT CHECK domain IN ('mental','fitness','nutrition','sleep','financial'), title, content_url)
- engagement_events (id, employee_id, module_id, action TEXT, created_at)
- recommendations (id, employee_id, module_id, score FLOAT, rank INT, generated_at)
Enable row-level security so each user reads only rows for their org, and employees read only their own rows. Add Stripe with three products: a $49 one-time starter, a per-seat subscription ($5-$20 per employee/month), and an annual enterprise SKU. Env vars: OPENAI_API_KEY, MERGE_API_KEY, TERRA_API_KEY, STRIPE_SECRET_KEY.
```

**2. Personalization Engine**

```text
Build the recommendation pipeline for WellnessIQ.
Input: an employee's assessment plus their engagement_events history.
Steps:
1. Use gpt-4o-mini with a strict JSON schema to turn free-text goals into structured tags (domains, intensity, constraints).
2. Score every module for that employee using tag match plus recent engagement (reward completed modules in preferred domains, decay ignored ones).
3. Write the top 8 modules to the recommendations table with score and rank.
Run this weekly via Vercel Cron for all active employees, and on demand right after a new assessment. Cache the gpt-4o-mini tag extraction so re-runs skip unchanged assessments to control cost.
```

**3. HRIS Onboarding + Manager Dashboard**

```text
Add one-click HR system onboarding using Merge.dev. Let an admin connect BambooHR, Gusto, Workday, or ADP through Merge Link, then sync employees (name, department, role_type) into the employees table, mapping Merge fields to our schema. Handle re-syncs idempotently by hris_id. Then build a manager dashboard showing, per department: active-employee rate, module completion rate by wellness domain, week-over-week engagement trend, and an ROI-style summary comparing engaged vs. disengaged cohorts. Keep all employee-level health data aggregated and anonymized in the manager view for privacy compliance.
```
