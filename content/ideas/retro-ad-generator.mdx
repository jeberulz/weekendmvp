## The Problem

Every DTC brand wants a 1972 print ad and a 1986 VHS bumper and they want it Tuesday. The art director quotes two weeks. Jasper will write the headline. Runway will motion the product. Canva will drop a "vintage" filter on a packshot. None of them know what a 1964 Volkswagen ad actually did with negative space, or why a 1991 Nike print still reads as Nike.

The result is AI slop with a grain overlay. The brand looks like it prompted "make it retro" and shipped. Historical reference is a craft. The tools treat it as a LUT.

In-house teams feel this as a briefing tax. The CMO wants a Super Bowl-adjacent nostalgia beat for a $40 SKU. The designer opens Midjourney, burns 40 gens, and still has to redraw the wordmark because the model invented a serif that never existed on the bottle. Freelance "vintage specialists" on the usual marketplaces quote $800–$2,500 for a single print system and still start from a Pinterest board, not a locked decade corpus. The gap is not "more images." It is a system that will not let the model wander.

## The Solution

A generator that starts from a dated reference system — decade, print vs TV, region, brand codes — then renders a modern product into that system. $39 one-off / $199 month in the one-liner. The ladder that matches the MCP: free demo → $49 for three ads → $199/month → $29 social add-on → $25k+/year if an in-house studio wants seats and a custom yearbook.

**How it works:**

1. **Yearbook** — Brand picks a decade + medium (1960s magazine, 1970s billboard, 1980s TV still, 1990s catalog). The app shows 8 locked references (public-domain or licensed scans), not a moodboard scrape.
2. **Product lock** — Upload the SKU, hex codes, and a one-line claim. The model is not allowed to invent a new logo or a fake celebrity.
3. **Export pack** — 4:5, 1:1, 16:9, plus a print PDF with bleed. Optional $29 add-on writes three platform captions in the same voice as the ad.

## Market Research

AI image generators: $349.6 million in 2023 to $1.08 billion by 2030 (17.7% CAGR, Grand View). A Dimension Market Research cut puts the same category at $1.88 billion by 2033 (18.1%). AI marketing spend was cited at $27.83 billion in 2024 to $35.54 billion in 2025 in the Ideabrowser pack — treat that as directional, it ages fast.

The category is crowded at "make an image." It is empty at "make this SKU look like a 1974 American print ad without hallucinating a wordmark." That constraint is the product.

Why now: Firefly, Flux, and GPT-Image finally hold a product lock if you few-shot them hard. That was not true in 2023. The cultural timing is the same wave that made "heritage" and "archive drops" a default DTC brief — except most of those campaigns are a brown overlay. Brands that can show the yearbook (here are the eight 1974 refs we locked) win the legal and the taste argument. Brands that cannot will keep shipping grain.

- **Runway** — from $15/month. Video-first. Wrong medium for a print yearbook.

- **Adobe Firefly / Sensei** — inside Creative Cloud. The art director already has it. They still do not have a dated reference lock.
- **DALL-E 3** — substitute. Same hallucination problem on logos and period type.

Free demo (watermarked, one decade) → $49 starter (3 ads) → $199/month (unlimited drafts, 4 decades) → $29 social caption add-on → custom yearbooks for $25k+/year. Jasper keeps the blog posts. ## Recommended Tech Stack

- **App:** Next.js 15 on Vercel
- **Auth / billing:** Clerk + Stripe
- **DB:** Supabase — `brands`, `yearbooks`, `renders`, `exports`
- **Models:** Flux or GPT-Image with a tight style adapter per decade; Claude for the claim line only
- **Refs:** your own scanned public-domain corpus in R2 — not a random Pinterest scrape
- **Export:** sharp for print PDF bleed

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of the "Retro Ad Generator": pick a decade and a medium, add your product photo, logo, colors and one line of copy, and get retro-style ads where the artwork is generated and your logo, product and words are placed by code, so they are never redrawn or misspelled.

Stack: Next.js (App Router, TypeScript), Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Storage, Auth with email magic link), an image generation API (Replicate with a Flux model) for backdrop artwork only, sharp to compose and pdf-lib to build the print PDF. Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on, each user reads only their own rows; yearbooks are read-only):
- brands(id, user_id, name, hex_colors text[], logo_path)
- yearbooks(id, decade, medium, style_prompt, reference_notes)
- renders(id, user_id, brand_id, yearbook_id, product_path, claim, status, output_paths jsonb, created_at)
- exports(id, render_id, ratio, path, created_at)

Screens: /login, /brand (logo, colors), /new (pick a yearbook, add the product and the line), /renders/[id] (four options, pick one, export).
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), REPLICATE_API_TOKEN.

Do not build: billing, credits or plans, a caption writer, watermarks, a scanned reference corpus, living artists or brands as style references.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the four tables exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves the Retro Ad Generator: the period look comes from a model, and everything that must be exact is placed by code.
1. Seed two yearbooks, a 1960s magazine page and a 1980s television still, each with a style prompt you wrote in your own words about layout, palette and printing look. Never name a living artist, a celebrity or a real brand in a style prompt.
2. The new-ad form takes a product photo, the brand's logo and hex colors, a one-line claim, a decade and a medium.
3. Ask the image API for 4 backdrop images from the style prompt, with an instruction that the image has no text, no logos and no people's faces. Reject any request whose claim text names a celebrity or a brand the user does not own, using a short deny list you keep in one file, and say why.
4. Compose each option in code with sharp: the backdrop, the product photo on a period-style panel, the logo placed exactly as uploaded and the claim set in a period-appropriate bundled font with the brand's hex colors. The model never draws the logo, the product or the words.
5. Export 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9 images, and a print PDF at 300 dpi with 0.125 inch bleed built with pdf-lib.
6. Test that the logo region of every composed output is pixel-identical to the uploaded logo after scaling, and that the PDF page size equals the trim size plus the bleed.
7. Limit a run to 4 generations to keep the cost known, and show the count used.
Rules: refuse a real person's likeness and a brand mark the user does not own. Label the art "generated in the style of the period". Never claim the ad is from the period.
Empty state: with no brand, show the brand form and the two yearbook previews.
Done when: four options render, the logo test passes, a claim naming a celebrity is refused, all three image sizes and the PDF export with the right bleed.
```
