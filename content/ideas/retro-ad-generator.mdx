## The Problem

Every DTC brand wants a 1972 print ad and a 1986 VHS bumper and they want it Tuesday. The art director quotes two weeks. Jasper will write the headline. Runway will motion the product. Canva will drop a "vintage" filter on a packshot. None of them know what a 1964 Volkswagen ad actually did with negative space, or why a 1991 Nike print still reads as Nike.

The result is AI slop with a grain overlay. The brand looks like it prompted "make it retro" and shipped. Historical reference is a craft. The tools treat it as a LUT.

In-house teams feel this as a briefing tax. The CMO wants a Super Bowl-adjacent nostalgia beat for a $40 SKU. The designer opens Midjourney, burns 40 gens, and still has to redraw the wordmark because the model invented a serif that never existed on the bottle. Freelance "vintage specialists" on the usual marketplaces quote $800–$2,500 for a single print system and still start from a Pinterest board, not a locked decade corpus. The gap is not "more images." It is a system that will not let the model wander.

## The Solution

A generator that starts from a dated reference system — decade, print vs TV, region, brand codes — then renders a modern product into that system. $39 one-off / $199 month in the one-liner. The ladder that matches the MCP: free demo → $49 for three ads → $199/month → $29 social add-on → $25k+/year if an in-house studio wants seats and a custom yearbook.

**How it works:**

1. **Yearbook** — Brand picks a decade + medium (1960s magazine, 1970s billboard, 1980s TV still, 1990s catalog). The app shows 8 locked references (public-domain or licensed scans), not a moodboard scrape.
2. **Product lock** — Upload the SKU, hex codes, and a one-line claim. The model is not allowed to invent a new logo or a fake celebrity.
3. **Export pack** — 4:5, 1:1, 16:9, plus a print PDF with bleed. Optional $29 add-on writes three platform captions in the same voice as the ad.

## Market Research

AI image generators: $349.6 million in 2023 to $1.08 billion by 2030 (17.7% CAGR, Grand View). A Dimension Market Research cut puts the same category at $1.88 billion by 2033 (18.1%). AI marketing spend was cited at $27.83 billion in 2024 to $35.54 billion in 2025 in the Ideabrowser pack — treat that as directional, it ages fast.

The category is crowded at "make an image." It is empty at "make this SKU look like a 1974 American print ad without hallucinating a wordmark." That constraint is the product.

Why now: Firefly, Flux, and GPT-Image finally hold a product lock if you few-shot them hard. That was not true in 2023. The cultural timing is the same wave that made "heritage" and "archive drops" a default DTC brief — except most of those campaigns are a brown overlay. Brands that can show the yearbook (here are the eight 1974 refs we locked) win the legal and the taste argument. Brands that cannot will keep shipping grain.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Jasper** — from $49/month. Copy plus generic visuals. No historical system.
- **Runway** — from $15/month. Video-first. Wrong medium for a print yearbook.
- **Canva AI** — freemium. "Vintage" is a slider. 125 million users will never need you; the 200 brands that care about craft will.
- **Adobe Firefly / Sensei** — inside Creative Cloud. The art director already has it. They still do not have a dated reference lock.
- **DALL-E 3** — substitute. Same hallucination problem on logos and period type.

## Recommended Tech Stack

- **App:** Next.js 15 on Vercel
- **Auth / billing:** Clerk + Stripe
- **DB:** Supabase — `brands`, `yearbooks`, `renders`, `exports`
- **Models:** Flux or GPT-Image with a tight style adapter per decade; Claude for the claim line only
- **Refs:** your own scanned public-domain corpus in R2 — not a random Pinterest scrape
- **Export:** sharp for print PDF bleed
