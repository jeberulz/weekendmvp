## The Problem

High-ticket freelancers still treat "getting found" as a Google SEO project. The buyer already left Google. A 2026 BrightLocal survey found AI recommendations jumped from 6% to 45% year over year as a way people discover local and professional services. ChatGPT crossed 400 million weekly users in February 2025 and 900 million a year later. Adobe's 2026 consumer-trends report says 25% of people now name AI as their top research tool, and 42% of those AI researchers always or frequently start there.

That is a hiring channel your Upwork profile and your "best copywriter in Austin" blog post do not cover. When a founder types "who should I hire for a $15k brand sprint," the model answers with a name — or it answers with a generic category and three agencies that published enough third-party mentions to look like the default. Eight tools already score how visible a *brand* is inside ChatGPT. None of them exist to put a *named person* into that answer.

Freelancers feel this as silence, not a dashboard. Referrals used to be the whole funnel. Referrals still close, but the shortlist is now assembled by a model that has never seen your case study PDF. Agencies will sell "GEO retainers" from $1,500 to $25,000 a month. Fiverr will sell a one-off "get listed on ChatGPT" gig for $30 to $3,000. Neither is built for a solo operator who needs three named placements, not an enterprise visibility score.

## The Solution

Ship a 21-day placement sprint that treats the freelancer as the product. The app inventories the three prompts their ideal client actually asks, finds the sources those models already cite, and manufactures the missing mentions — guest quotes, comparison pages, niche directories, podcast show notes — until the name appears in the answer.

Do not build another Profound clone. Profound sells Fortune 500 dashboards. You sell a named person showing up when someone asks who to hire.

**How it works:**

1. **Prompt map** — The freelancer pastes their offer, niche, and three recent wins. The app expands that into the 10–15 buyer questions ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini already answer in their category, then records the current cited sources.
2. **Placement sprint** — Over 21 days the system drafts and places three assets on sites those models already trust: a comparison blurb, a quoted expert inset, a directory or podcast page. Each placement is tracked against the original prompts.
3. **Named-in-ChatGPT retainer** — Weekly re-checks confirm the name still appears. When a placement drops, the queue writes the replacement instead of sending another "your visibility score is 34" email.

The TAM is not "SEO software." It is high-ticket independents — consultants, designers, copywriters, fractional operators — whose pipeline used to be warm intro and is now a model shortlist. Forbes ran William Arruda in August 2026 on personal-brand visibility inside AI answers. That is the buyer education you ride, not a keyword you invent.

BrightLocal's 2026 AI consumer-adoption numbers are the closest published proxy for "will a stranger trust a model to pick a pro." Combined with Adobe's 25% / 42% research-start figures, the demand side is already here. The supply side is enterprise GEO platforms and cheap Fiverr gigs. The $299–$499 freelancer band is empty.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Profound** — $99 Starter, custom Enterprise. $155 million raised. Built for Fortune 500 brand teams who want a visibility score across models. They will not staff a 21-day sprint for a $400/hour designer.
- **Peec** — $80–$205 per month. Three thousand-plus brands. Closest incumbent on price. Still a brand dashboard, not a named-person placement engine.
- **Semrush AI Toolkit** — $99 per month per domain, 25 prompts. Fine if you already live in Semrush. Useless if you do not have a domain worth monitoring and just need your name in three answers.
- **Rankability** — $99–$399 per month. Content-ops overlay on GEO. Same problem: they optimize pages, not people.
- **Ahrefs Brand Radar** — checker is free; the paid suite is an SEO platform. Good for the audit lead magnet. Bad as the product.
- **GEO agencies** — $1,500–$25,000+ per month. Overkill for a solo freelancer. **Fiverr GEO gigs** — $30–$3,000 per project, typical tickets around $156–$291. Inconsistent, untracked, and they vanish after delivery.

**Your Opportunity**

Sell the placement, not the score. Free AI Visibility Audit (Ahrefs-style checker plus three prompt screenshots) → $299 Placement Sprint (21 days, three placements, money-back if the name never appears) → $149/month Named In ChatGPT retainer → $499/month Pro for operators who want prompt expansion and monthly new placements. Profound and Peec will keep the brand logos. You keep the people those brands hire.

Unit economics (month 6 napkin, not a forecast):

- 10% close to a sprint → 3 sprints → $897
- Half of sprint buyers stay on the $149 retainer → about $2,600 blended monthly once the base compounds

Do not launch a $99/month dashboard. The audit is the wedge. The sprint is the cash. The retainer is the business.

## Recommended Tech Stack

- **App:** Next.js 15 + TypeScript on Vercel
- **Auth / billing:** Clerk + Stripe Checkout (one-time sprint) + Stripe Billing (retainers)
- **DB:** Convex or Supabase — `freelancers`, `prompt_clusters`, `placements`, `rechecks`
- **Models:** OpenAI + Anthropic for draft placements; Perplexity / ChatGPT browsing via a thin scrape-and-diff job for the weekly re-check
- **Outreach:** Instantly or a Gmail API queue for the three placement pitches
- **Email:** Resend for audit delivery

## AI Prompts to Build This

Copy and paste these into Claude, Cursor, or your favorite AI tool.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of the "AI Search Publicist": a freelancer lists the questions their ideal client asks an AI search tool, the app checks whether their name appears in the answers and which sources are cited, and it drafts three honest pieces for the freelancer to publish themselves. It measures; it promises nothing.

Stack: Next.js (App Router, TypeScript), Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Auth with email magic link), the Perplexity API for answers with their cited sources, the Anthropic API to propose questions and write drafts. Everything shown comes from a real check you can repeat. Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on, each user reads only their own rows):
- profiles(user_id, display_name, offer, niche, case_study_text)
- prompts(id, user_id, question, approved boolean, created_at)
- checks(id, prompt_id, checked_at, name_present boolean, cited_sources jsonb, answer_excerpt)
- placements(id, user_id, kind, target, draft, status, published_url, created_at)  kind is comparison_blurb, expert_quote or directory_page; status is draft, published or dropped

Screens: /login, /profile (offer, niche, pasted case studies), /prompts (propose, approve and check), /results (name present or absent, with sources), /placements.
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), PERPLEXITY_API_KEY, ANTHROPIC_API_KEY.

Do not build: billing, sprint pricing or retainers, a money-back guarantee, automated outreach or posting, a weekly cron, a visibility score, screenshots from other AI tools.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the four tables exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves the Publicist: an honest map of where the freelancer's name is and is not, and drafts that only say what is true.
1. The profile takes the offer, the niche and the pasted text of up to three real case studies.
2. Ask the Anthropic API for 10 to 15 questions a buyer would ask an AI search tool when looking for someone like this, as JSON { questions: string[] }. The freelancer approves up to 10.
3. Check each approved question with the Perplexity API. Save the answer excerpt and the cited sources (domain and address). name_present is true only when the freelancer's exact name appears in the answer text, case-insensitive. Do not calculate a score; show present or absent for each question and the sources that were cited.
4. For the questions where the name is absent, list the sources that were cited, since those are where a real mention would have to appear.
5. Draft three pieces from the case-study text only: a short comparison blurb, an expert quote and a page for a niche directory or a podcast guest. Check in code that every number and client name in a draft appears in the pasted case studies. Drop any claim that does not.
6. The freelancer publishes the drafts themselves, then marks each placement published with its address. A Re-check button runs the checks again and shows what changed since last time: names that appeared, names that disappeared and new sources. A disappeared name opens a task to replace the placement.
Rules: never create a fake review, persona or mention, and never post or send anything for the freelancer. Say that no one can promise an AI tool will name you.
Empty state: before a profile exists, show the profile form.
Done when: a sample profile produces questions the freelancer can approve, a check records present or absent with cited sources, a draft containing an invented number is dropped and a re-check lists a name that appeared since the first run.
```

**3. Landing Page**

```text
Build a one-page landing site for the AI Search Publicist, a way for freelancers to see and improve how they show up in AI answers.
Hero: "Find out if AI search recommends you, and what it cites instead." Sub: "Check the questions your clients ask, see whether your name appears and get honest drafts for the places that would earn a real mention." One button: Join the waitlist.
Sections: a sample results table with present and absent rows and their sources, how it works in four steps (map the questions, check, draft, re-check), and an FAQ on what is promised (a measurement, never a placement), why nothing is posted for you and which tools are checked.
Waitlist: store the email in a waitlist table in Supabase. No other service.
Style: Geist, an off-white background, near-black type, one blue accent.
Done when: the page renders on a phone and a submitted email appears in the waitlist table.
```

Brand for the AI Search Publicist: pick a short working name, then design a wordmark and an icon that suggest a speech bubble with a name tag.
Colors: off-white, near-black and one blue. Type: Geist.
Deliverables: wordmark, icon, two result chips (name present, name absent) that differ by shape as well as color, a results row layout that shows its sources, and one launch graphic.
