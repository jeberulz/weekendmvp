## The Problem

The prototype works. The core features are there. But something broke and you cannot figure out what. The AI keeps giving you the same wrong answer. Stack Overflow has nothing. The tutorial you followed is three months old and the API has changed twice since.

This is the new normal. Vibe coding lets anyone build fast. Cursor, Replit, Lovable, Bolt: idea to working prototype in a weekend. But the last stretch, the weird edge case, the integration that will not integrate, the error message that does not exist on Google, is where projects stall. And where they stay.

The painful part is how close these projects are. The builder is at 80 percent, blocked on a single concrete problem that an experienced developer would recognize in thirty seconds. They do not need a contractor for a two-week engagement or a course. They need fifteen minutes with someone who has already been past this exact wall.

## The Solution

Last 20 is the phone-a-friend for builders who are almost there. Upload your code, describe the block, and get matched with an expert who has hit that wall before and knows the way around it. Fifteen minutes on a screen share. Problem solved. Ship it.

Experts set their own rates and the platform takes a cut. Hobbyists pay per session for one-off rescues; agencies that cannot afford stalled client work pay an annual plan for priority access. The product is scoped intentionally narrow: not mentorship, not freelancing, just a fast, specific unblock at the moment of maximum frustration. Every rescue is a natural referral, because the builder just watched their dead project come back to life.

**How it works:**

1. Build the request flow: a builder uploads a code snippet or repo link, describes the block, and tags the stack so matching has signal.
2. Build matching and scheduling: route the request to available experts who know that stack and let one claim it for a 15-minute screen share.
3. Add payments and a post-session referral loop: experts set rates, the platform takes 10 percent, and a solved rescue prompts a share.

## Market Research

The supply of stuck builders is exploding precisely because the tools got good. Every new vibe coder who ships an 80-percent prototype is a future Last 20 customer the moment they hit a wall their AI cannot clear. The frustration is loud, public, and easy to find.

Growth lives where the frustration lives: Reddit threads full of anyone-else-hit-this-error, AI YouTube comment sections, and agency Slack channels asking whether anyone has shipped a given integration before. Start with a handful of experts and a handful of stuck builders, prove the rescue, and let every save turn into a referral.

- Vibe-coding tools have created a wave of builders who reach 80 percent and stall
- Reddit and Discord are full of recurring, specific anyone-hit-this-error posts
- Agencies lose real money to a single stalled client integration

## Competitive Landscape

The existing options are mismatched to the problem: too slow, too broad, or the very thing that already failed the builder. None deliver a fast, scoped, 15-minute unblock.

- **Upwork / Fiverr** (10-20% fees) — Built for full projects with onboarding and proposals. Far too slow and heavy for a single 15-minute block.
- **Stack Overflow** (Free) — Asynchronous and context-free. Useless when your error does not exist on Google and you need help now.
- **Codementor** (~$1/min) — Closest analog, but oriented to longer mentoring sessions rather than instant, scoped rescue at the moment of breakage.
- **ChatGPT / Claude** ($20/mo) — The thing that already failed them. Great for the first 80 percent, unreliable on the gnarly last 20.

**Your Opportunity**

No platform is purpose-built for the instant, scoped, 15-minute rescue of a vibe-coding builder stuck on one concrete block. The two-sided liquidity is the hard part, but the narrow scope makes each match fast and the value obvious.

## Business Model

The model is a take rate on expert-set pricing, with an annual plan for the buyers who feel the pain most. Hobbyists pay per rescue; agencies pay yearly for priority access because one stalled client build costs more than the plan.

A blended take of roughly 10 to 20 dollars per session means a few hundred rescues a month, or a modest base of agency plans, clears 5K. The honest risk is marketplace liquidity: you must keep enough vetted experts online that a stuck builder gets matched quickly. Seed supply first, concentrate demand in one stack, and expand once the match time is consistently low.

- **Hobbyist** ($15-50/session) — Pay per 15-minute rescue. Expert sets the rate; the platform takes 10 percent.
- **Agency** ($99-499/yr) — Priority matching and a roster of vetted experts for teams that cannot afford stalled client work.
- **Expert** (Keep 90%) — Experts set their own rates and earn from rescues between or alongside their main work.

**Unit Economics**

- **10%** — Platform take rate
- **~300** — Rescues/mo to ~$5K
- **15 min** — Typical session length
- **10 hrs** — Weekend MVP scope

## Recommended Tech Stack

The MVP is a scheduling and payments marketplace, not heavy engineering. The early bottleneck is trust and match speed, so build the request and matching loop first and keep everything else minimal.

- **Next.js + Supabase** (App) — Auth, request storage, and expert profiles with minimal backend so you can focus on the matching loop.
- **Stack tags + availability queue** (Matching) — Routes each block to online experts who know that stack; simple to start, easy to tune as volume grows.
- **Embedded screen share (Daily / Whereby)** (Sessions) — Drop-in 15-minute video with screen share without building real-time infrastructure yourself.
- **Stripe Connect** (Payments) — Expert payouts and the platform take rate handled with marketplace-ready billing primitives.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of "Last 20": a builder who is stuck near the end of a project posts the block, an expert with the right skills claims it, and they agree a 15-minute rescue call. Sessions are free during the pilot and no money moves through the app.

Stack: Next.js (App Router, TypeScript), Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Auth with email magic link), Resend for the notification and confirmation emails. The call itself uses a meeting link the expert pastes in. Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on; a builder reads their own requests, experts read open requests that match their skills and their own claims):
- profiles(user_id, role, display_name, stack_tags text[], available boolean)  role is builder or expert
- requests(id, builder_id, title, description, repo_url, snippet, stack_tags text[], status, claimed_by, meeting_url, scheduled_at, created_at)  status is open, claimed, scheduled, done or cancelled
- claims(id, request_id, expert_id, claimed_at)  unique on request_id
- session_feedback(id, request_id, resolved boolean, rating, note)

Screens: /login, /requests/new, /requests/[id] (status, the meeting link and feedback), /queue (experts), /me.
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), RESEND_API_KEY.

Do not build: billing, expert rates or a platform fee, Stripe Connect or payouts, an embedded video call, an agency plan, referral loops.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the four tables exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves Last 20: a stuck builder gets a real person in a short call, fast, without leaking a secret.
1. The request form takes a title, a description of the block, stack tags, an optional repo link and an optional snippet of up to 8,000 characters. Scan the snippet and the description for strings that look like keys or tokens and block the submit with a message that names what was found.
2. When a request is created, email the available experts whose stack tags overlap at least one of the request's tags, through Resend. The email shows the title and tags, not the snippet.
3. The queue shows matching open requests to experts. An expert presses Claim. Insert a claims row; the unique key means two experts claiming at once leave one winner and one clear refusal. Set the request to claimed and email both sides.
4. The expert proposes a time within the next 24 hours and pastes a meeting link. The builder confirms, which sets scheduled and emails both with the details.
5. After the time passes, the builder answers whether it was resolved and gives a 1 to 5 rating and a note. Show each expert's resolved rate and rating on their profile with the number of sessions behind them.
Rules: never ask for credentials or access to a repository. Say on the request page that sessions are free in the pilot and any payment is between the two people. Show the snippet only to the claiming expert.
Empty state: an empty queue says no one is stuck right now and shows the expert profile form.
Done when: a snippet containing a fake key is blocked, matching experts get one email each, two simultaneous claims produce one claim, the meeting is confirmed by both and the expert's resolved rate updates after feedback.
```
