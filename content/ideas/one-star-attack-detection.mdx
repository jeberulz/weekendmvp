## The Problem

A restaurant's Google rating drops from 4.8 to 3.9 overnight. Ten one-star reviews from brand-new accounts, identical phrasing, posted between 2 a.m. and 5 a.m. Morning coffee is when a regular mentions the drop. By then customers book elsewhere. Platform appeals take weeks. Revenue bleeds in hours.

Enterprise ORM suites (Podium ~$399/month, Chatmeter, Birdeye) bury alerts inside marketing dashboards built for multi-location brands. ReviewPush and Reputology offer real-time alerts from roughly $39–$49/month but still optimize for "every new review," not coordinated attack patterns. Native Google/Yelp emails are easy to miss. Single-location owners need one job: SMS when a rating is under attack, with evidence and a 10-minute response playbook.

## The Solution

Ratingwire monitors Google and Yelp listings for review velocity, account age proxies, and sentiment shocks. When patterns break — cluster of new accounts, sentiment crash, 3× review speed — an SMS fires within minutes with dispute templates and suggested replies. No enterprise dashboard required.

**How it works:**

1. **Link listing + phone** — Connect Google Business Profile / Yelp; verify SMS number.
2. **Baseline learning** — First two weeks learn normal review velocity for that location.
3. **Anomaly detect** — Score spikes: volume, one-star clustering, text similarity, off-hours bursts.
4. **Instant SMS** — Alert with links, evidence bullets, and copy-paste dispute/response templates.
5. **Incident log** — Timestamped record for platform appeals and agency handoff.

Weekend MVP: polling Google/Yelp data sources + Twilio SMS + threshold rules. Tune false positives with 10 restaurants before opening signup.

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves Ratingwire: a detector that catches a real attack and stays quiet on an ordinary bad evening.
1. Import a CSV with columns external_id, rating, text, created_at for one location. Skip rows already imported.
2. Compute the baseline from the previous 14 days: reviews per day and average rating. Never let the baseline fall below 0.5 reviews a day, so a quiet shop cannot divide by almost zero.
3. Write the detector as a pure function over the reviews and a time. It fires when there are at least 5 reviews in the last 6 hours (a minimum volume), and both the count is 3 times the baseline rate or more and 70 percent or more of them are one star, or the average rating over 24 hours has dropped 0.4 or more. The sensitivity setting scales those thresholds.
4. Add evidence: the counts, the one-star share, the rating before and after, and any group of 3 or more new reviews with near-identical text (word overlap of 0.8 or more). Do not store reviewer names.
5. Deduplicate: no second incident for the same location within 12 hours.
6. On an incident, text the owner the rating change, the number of new one-star reviews and a link to /incidents/[id], which shows a dispute template and a suggested public reply filled in from the evidence. Hold the text until quiet hours end unless always_alert is on. Send only to an opted-in number and honor STOP by clearing sms_opted_in_at.
7. A "This was fine" button on an incident sets resolved_as to fine and makes that location's detector 25 percent less sensitive, down to a floor.
Rules: three honest one-star reviews in an evening rush must never fire an incident (test it). Say plainly that alerts are signals, not proof.
Empty state: with no reviews, show the CSV columns and a sample file with a 12-review attack and a quiet evening.
Done when: replaying the sample creates exactly one incident at the attack and none during the quiet evening, one text is sent to an opted-in number, a second burst inside 12 hours adds no incident, and This was fine lowers the sensitivity.
```

False positives kill this product. A normal bad-dinner rush of three honest one-stars must not look like an attack. Tunable sensitivity, quiet hours, and a "this was fine" button that trains the model are mandatory before paid launch. Lead with restaurants and salons — high review volume and clear revenue sensitivity — then expand to dentists and home services.

Agency channel: white-label SMS that says "via Your Agency Review Defense." Agencies already sell reputation retainers; giving them an attack detector increases perceived responsiveness without building ML. Compliance: SMS opt-in, Google/Yelp API ToS, and careful storage of review text. Market with incident postmortems ("How a taco shop caught a 12-review bomb at 3 a.m.") rather than feature matrices against Podium.

Price annually at a discount ($100/year) for owners who hate monthly cards. Churn will cluster after quiet months; counter with a monthly "all clear" SMS so the product stays visible when attacks do not.
