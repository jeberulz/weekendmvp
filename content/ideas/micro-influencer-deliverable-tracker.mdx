## The Problem

A brand marketing manager greenlights a 40-creator micro-influencer campaign for a product launch. Contracts get sent as PDF attachments in Gmail. Deliverables — one Reel, two Stories, one static post — get tracked in a shared Google Sheet that three people edit simultaneously. Content approvals happen in a Slack channel that scrolls past anyone who steps away for a day. Payments go out via PayPal, one at a time, whenever someone remembers to check who actually posted. By week three, nobody can answer a simple question with confidence: which of the 40 creators have delivered everything they promised, and which of them have already been paid.

This is not a hypothetical. Brands now run micro-influencer programs at real scale — the average brand works with about 30 micro-influencers per campaign, a 36% year-over-year increase, and nearly a fifth of brands manage 10 to 50 creators on a single campaign while another 15% run 50 to 100. Researchers who study this shift are blunt about what breaks first: managing relationships at scale is the most common operational challenge, and once a team coordinates 20 or 30 creators simultaneously, manual tracking through spreadsheets breaks down into missed contracts, delayed payments, and scattered performance data.

The payment side cuts both ways. A survey of 500 influencers found 87% have experienced late or mishandled payments, and 55% say they would not work with a brand again after being paid late. On the brand side, the same disorganization that delays a payment also creates duplicate payments, because nobody has a single source of truth showing a creator was already paid for that deliverable through a different channel. A team running a 50-creator campaign on spreadsheets and DMs isn't just risking creator goodwill — it's risking real budget leakage and a compliance headache when finance asks for a clean record of what was promised, delivered, and paid.

The tools brands already own don't solve this. Project-management software has no concept of a creator contract or a payment trigger; accounting software has no concept of a content deliverable. The gap between "what 50 creators owe us" and "what we owe 50 creators" is exactly where a purpose-built tracker earns its keep.

## The Solution

A lightweight, brand-side campaign command center built specifically around the three things a micro-influencer campaign actually needs to track: the contract terms per creator, the approval status of each individual deliverable, and the payment trigger tied to that approval. Instead of a generic project board, every creator gets a row with their agreed deliverables (one Reel, two Stories, whatever the contract specifies), and every deliverable moves through a fixed pipeline — not started, submitted, needs revision, approved, paid. When a deliverable hits "approved," the tool automatically queues the payment tied to it, so payment timing is a direct consequence of delivery instead of a separate manual step someone has to remember.

**How it works:**

1. **Add the campaign and creators** — Import a creator roster (CSV or manual entry), attach a contract template per creator with agreed deliverables, rate, and due dates; e-signature request goes out automatically.
2. **Track deliverables against the contract** — Each creator gets a personal upload link (no login required) to submit proof of posting — a screenshot, video file, or live URL — against each line item in their contract.
3. **Approve or request revisions** — The brand reviews submitted content against the contract terms in one queue; approve, leave inline feedback, or request a revision, with an automatic reminder sent to the creator if a deadline is approaching.
4. **Trigger payment on approval** — Approving the final deliverable on a creator's contract flags them for payout; a batched payment run (or a Stripe Connect payout) processes on a schedule the brand controls, with every payment stamped to the deliverable that earned it.

The dashboard view is the whole point: a single screen showing all 50 creators, color-coded by status, so a two-person marketing team can answer "who's late, who's owed money, and who do we chase today" in under a minute instead of cross-referencing three tools.

## Market Research

The category this product sits inside is large and growing quickly, and the specific pain it solves — campaign-management chaos at the micro-influencer tier — is well documented and under-served by the platforms built for it.

- **The overall influencer marketing industry is valued at roughly $40.51 billion in 2026, up from $31.07 billion in 2025** — expanding fast enough that even a thin operational slice is a meaningful opportunity ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/influencer-marketing-market)).
- **The influencer marketing platform/software segment was valued at roughly $26.15 billion in 2025, projected to reach $197.69 billion by 2035** at a 22.42% CAGR — brands are shifting budget from ad-hoc management toward dedicated software ([SNS Insider, via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/influencer-marketing-platform-market-size-103000913.html)).
- **Brands now run an average of 30 micro-influencers per campaign, a 36% year-over-year increase**, with 19% managing 10-50 creators and 15.2% managing 50-100 — exactly the headcount range where spreadsheets stop working ([Statusphere, State of Micro-Influencer Marketing 2025](https://brands.joinstatus.com/micro-influencer-report-2025)).
- **87% of creators surveyed have experienced late or mishandled payments, and 55% would decline to work with that brand again** — payment reliability directly drives whether a brand can re-book its best creators next quarter ([Lumanu, Insights from 500 Influencers](https://www.lumanu.com/blog/insights-from-500-influencers-on-their-payment-experience)).
- **Managing relationships at scale is the most common operational challenge in influencer marketing**, with spreadsheet tracking breaking down once a team crosses into the 20-30 creator range ([Popular Pays](https://popularpays.com/blog/solving-your-biggest-influencer-marketing-challenges-what-marketers-really-want)).

Taken together, the market is large and growing, but the operational layer for the 10-100 creator range most brands actually run in is still dominated by spreadsheets, Slack, and PayPal rather than anything built for the contract-to-payment lifecycle.

## Competitive Landscape

The influencer marketing software category is real and well-funded, but almost every established platform is priced for enterprise teams running six-figure annual programs — not a small team running one 50-creator launch campaign on a limited budget.

- **GRIN** — The most complete end-to-end platform (relationships, seeding, content, payments), but priced for scale: published tiers run Lite at $399/mo for 15 creators up to Complete at $1,799/mo for 400, and the Core enterprise plan is commonly quoted around $25,000/year ([GRIN pricing, via StackScored](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/influencer-marketing/grin/)).
- **Aspire (formerly AspireIQ)** — Strong creator-community and content-library features, but pricing starts around $2,000-2,300/month on a 12-month contract, landing near $30,000/year minimum before onboarding fees ([Aspire pricing review](https://www.influencer-hero.com/blogs/aspire-pricing-and-review-features-costs-and-benefits)).
- **Upfluence** — Solid ecommerce integrations and AI-driven outreach, priced roughly $478-$2,000/month on annual contracts — mid-market pricing that assumes a dedicated influencer-marketing hire ([Influencer analytics comparison](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-analytics-tools-comparison/)).
- **CreatorIQ** — The enterprise standard for full campaign lifecycle management, custom-priced from roughly $35,000/year up to $200,000+/year for enterprise deployments — built for Fortune 500 brand teams ([CreatorIQ alternatives, Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/creatoriq-alternatives)).
- **Modash** — A creator discovery and analytics database (350M+ profiles) starting around $99-299/month, but it's a search and vetting tool, not a contract or payment tracker — brands still export to a spreadsheet once they move into execution ([Modash alternatives comparison](https://dupple.com/learn/best-influencer-marketing-platforms)).

**Your Opportunity:** every incumbent above is priced for a dedicated influencer-marketing budget line and a person whose full-time job is running the platform. None will move down to a $29-79/month plan built for a two-person team running one campaign at a time — it would cannibalize their enterprise contracts. The wedge: skip discovery and outreach entirely (brands already know who they're working with) and build the best contract-to-payment layer for the 10-100 creator range, at a price a small team can expense without a procurement process.

## Business Model

Subscription SaaS priced per creator-month, with a free tier that lets a small team run one real campaign before hitting a wall. The core workload is CRUD, file storage, e-signature, and scheduled payouts — not heavy AI inference — so gross margin stays high even at low price points; the main variable costs are Stripe Connect fees and file storage for submitted content proof.

- **Free** ($0) — 1 active campaign, up to 10 creators, manual payment tracking (no automated payout), contract templates, deliverable checklist
- **Growth** ($39/mo) — Up to 100 creators across unlimited campaigns, e-signature contracts, automated payment triggers via Stripe Connect, deliverable reminders, CSV export for finance
- **Agency** ($99/mo) — Multiple brand workspaces, white-labeled creator upload links, team seats with approval permissions, payment batching and reconciliation reports, priority support

**Unit Economics**

- **~$0.60/creator/mo** — Blended cost (Stripe Connect fees + file storage + email/SMS reminders)
- **~85%** — Gross margin at the Growth tier
- **$40-70** — Target CAC (content marketing + influencer-agency partnerships)
- **~$470** — 12-month LTV at $39/mo with modest churn improvement after the first paid campaign

At $39/mo, reaching $10K MRR requires roughly 260 paying teams — realistic for a product that sells itself inside marketing and creator-economy communities where the pain (missed deliverables, late payments) is a recurring, visible complaint rather than a hypothetical one.

## Recommended Tech Stack

This is a workflow and payments app, not an AI product — AI is a helper for content review, not the core mechanic. Optimize for reliable state transitions, auditable payment history, and a frictionless no-login upload flow for creators who will never make an account.

- **Next.js 14 (App Router) + Vercel** — Brand-facing dashboard plus public, token-based creator upload pages that require no login, since creators won't create accounts for a one-off campaign.
- **Postgres via Supabase** — Core tables: `campaigns`, `creators`, `contracts`, `deliverables` (status enum), `submissions`, `payments`. Row-level security scoped per brand workspace.
- **Stripe Connect (Express accounts)** — Each creator onboards a lightweight Express account to receive payouts; payments trigger programmatically the moment a deliverable's status flips to "approved," with a full transfer log for reconciliation.
- **PandaDoc or Documenso API** — Contract generation and e-signature per creator, pre-filled from a campaign template; signed status syncs back to the contract record automatically.
- **Claude or GPT-4o-mini (vision)** — Reviews submitted screenshots/video against the contract's stated requirements and flags likely mismatches for the human approver — a second set of eyes, never an auto-approve.
- **Resend + Twilio** — Email for contract requests and payment receipts; SMS reminders as deadlines approach, since creators check texts faster than a project-management app.

## AI Prompts to Build This

Copy and paste these into Claude, Cursor, or your favorite AI tool.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Create a Next.js 14 (App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind) app called "DeliverTrack" for brands running micro-influencer campaigns. Provision Supabase Postgres with these tables:

- workspaces (id, name, owner_user_id, plan TEXT default 'free')
- campaigns (id, workspace_id, name, start_date, end_date, status)
- creators (id, campaign_id, name, email, phone, instagram_handle, tiktok_handle, stripe_connect_account_id)
- contracts (id, creator_id, deliverables JSONB, rate_cents INT, usage_rights TEXT, due_date, signed_at, pandadoc_document_id)
- deliverables (id, contract_id, description, status TEXT CHECK status IN ('not_started','submitted','needs_revision','approved','paid'), due_date)
- submissions (id, deliverable_id, file_url, post_url, submitted_at, reviewer_note)
- payments (id, deliverable_id, amount_cents, stripe_transfer_id, paid_at)

Enable row-level security scoped by workspace_id via a workspace_members join table. Set up Stripe Connect (Express) onboarding for creators and Stripe Billing for the workspace subscription (Free/Growth $39/Agency $99).
```

**2. Deliverable Pipeline + No-Login Creator Upload**

```text
Build the deliverable tracking pipeline and a public, token-based upload page that requires no creator login.

Requirements:
- Each deliverable gets a unique, unguessable upload token generated on contract creation.
- The upload page shows the creator their deliverable list and due dates, with an upload widget (drag-and-drop file or paste a live post URL) per item.
- On submit, update deliverable status to 'submitted', store the file/URL in a submissions row, and notify the brand via email.
- Build a brand-side review queue listing all 'submitted' deliverables across creators, with Approve / Request Revision buttons.
- Approve: set status to 'approved', create a payments row in 'pending' state, trigger the payment webhook.
- Request Revision: set status back to 'needs_revision', require a reviewer note, and re-email the creator their upload link with the note attached.
- Send an automated reminder (email + SMS via Twilio) 48 hours before any deliverable's due_date if it's still 'not_started'.
```

**3. AI Content Review Assistant**

```text
Add an AI pre-check that runs when a creator submits a deliverable, before it reaches the human review queue.

Given: the contract's deliverable description (e.g. "1 Instagram Reel, must tag @brand and use #BrandLaunch, minimum 15 seconds") and the submitted content (image/video file or a fetched post URL with caption text).

Call a vision-capable model (Claude or GPT-4o-mini) with a strict JSON schema:
{ likely_compliant: boolean, missing_requirements: string[], confidence: number, notes: string }

Check for required hashtags/mentions in the caption, approximate video length if inferable, and whether the content matches the described format. Never auto-approve or auto-reject — surface the result as a badge ("AI flagged: missing #BrandLaunch tag") in the review queue. Log every check to an ai_reviews table for auditing.
```

**4. Payment Batching and Stripe Connect Payout**

```text
Implement the payment trigger and batch payout flow.

When a deliverable's status flips to 'approved': create a payments row in 'pending' status linked to that deliverable and the creator's stripe_connect_account_id, then add it to the workspace's next scheduled payout batch (default weekly, configurable).

Build a scheduled job (Vercel Cron, weekly) that:
1. Groups all 'pending' payments by workspace.
2. For each, calls stripe.transfers.create to the creator's Connect account for the deliverable's rate_cents, tagged with metadata for deliverable_id and campaign_id.
3. On success, updates the payment row to 'paid' with the stripe_transfer_id and paid_at timestamp, and marks the deliverable 'paid'.
4. On failure (e.g. creator hasn't finished Connect onboarding), leaves the payment 'pending' and notifies the workspace owner with the creator and reason.

Expose a CSV export endpoint so a brand's finance team can reconcile every payment against the deliverable that earned it.
```
