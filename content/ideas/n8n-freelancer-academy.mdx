## The Problem

A self-taught n8n freelancer lands their first automation client, quotes $400 for a project a veteran would have billed at $2,500, then spends the weekend digging through forum threads and half-finished YouTube tutorials to figure out why a webhook keeps timing out. The technical knowledge to *build* is scattered and free; the business knowledge to *charge* is locked inside the heads of people already earning $150 an hour who have no reason to write it down. That gap—between "I can wire up a workflow" and "I can run this as a profitable practice"—is where most n8n freelancers stall out, undercharge, and quietly go back to a day job.

The pain is loud and specific in the exact communities where these people already gather. Reddit's r/n8n has crossed 104,000 members, and "show me your best workflow" and "how do I price this?" threads routinely draw 50 to 120 comments of freelancers comparing notes because there is no authoritative source. The "n8n Automation Community" on Facebook has 74,000 members surfacing the same two questions on repeat: how do I find clients, and what do I charge them. YouTube creators like Nate Herk pull 200,000 to 450,000 views on n8n tutorials—overwhelming proof of demand—but the content skews beginner, tool-focused, and theory-heavy. Almost none of it teaches the messy middle: scoping a client engagement, writing the proposal, delivering under a deadline, and not leaving money on the table.

The downstream cost is a career that plateaus. A freelancer who never learns to price, scope, and sell stays stuck taking $400 jobs that should be $2,500 jobs, burns out on volume, and never builds the referral flywheel that compounds into a real business. The automation itself is the easy part. The bridge from competent builder to well-paid specialist is the product nobody has built for this audience—and the audience is telling you, in six-figure community membership counts, exactly where they are standing.

## The Solution

A cohort-and-community academy that trains n8n freelancers on real client work, not toy demos—then hands them the business scaffolding (pricing templates, proposal scripts, a workflow vault, and a vetted job board) to actually get paid for it. Members join a tier, get live weekly build sessions against realistic client briefs, and graduate into a paid Workflow Vault of commercial-grade automations they can customize and ship the same day. The moat is not the video content, which commoditizes; it is the compounding network effect of a job board where trained freelancers meet businesses that need automation help, plus a certification that becomes the default trust signal in a market with no credential.

**How it works:**

1. **Join a tier** — Freelancer picks Membership ($49/mo), Agency Ready ($149/mo), or Elite ($499), gets instant access to the Workflow Vault and the private community on day one.
2. **Learn on real briefs** — Weekly live coding sessions rebuild actual client scenarios end to end; members submit their own workflows for auto-grading and mentor feedback.
3. **Ship faster** — Members clone commercial-grade workflows from the Vault, apply the included pricing and proposal templates, and deliver paid client work without reinventing each build.
4. **Get placed** — Certified members unlock the vetted job board where businesses post automation gigs; the platform takes a placement fee, closing the loop from training to income.

## Market Research

Two markets are compounding at once: the online-education market that sells the training, and the automation market that creates the demand for trained freelancers. That overlap is what makes the timing a wedge rather than a coin flip.

- **The global e-learning market is projected to grow from $218.9 billion in 2025 to $615.6 billion by 2033**, a CAGR in the 14 to 18.9 percent range (Cognitive Market Research; Future Market Insights). The United States alone is expected to generate roughly $74.8 billion in e-learning revenue in 2025—the tooling and buyer behavior for paid online skills training is fully mature.
- **The business process automation and RPA markets sit above $100 billion and are projected to roughly double by 2030** (Precedence Research), with the broader process-automation category forecast to reach $210 billion by 2037 from about $99.76 billion in 2025. Every dollar of that spend needs people who can implement it—and n8n is where a growing share of the open-source, cost-conscious segment lands.
- **Reddit's r/n8n has surpassed 104,000 members**, with workflow and pricing threads regularly exceeding 100 comments (Ideabrowser community analysis, idea #1680). This is not demand you have to create; it is demand already congregating and openly asking for exactly this product.
- **Facebook's "n8n Automation Community" holds 74,000 members**, dominated by freelancers, SMEs, and agency owners trading client-acquisition tactics and troubleshooting builds—a second, non-overlapping distribution channel of the same buyer.
- **Leading n8n YouTube creators average 200,000 to 450,000 views per tutorial** (Nate Herk, Jono Catliff), proving the audience will spend hours on automation content—yet the advanced, business-focused, "how to run this as a practice" tier of content is almost entirely missing.

The signal is unusually clean: massive and growing buyer intent, a fragmented supply of free-but-scattered content, and zero authoritative credential in a niche that is exploding.

## Competitive Landscape

The category splits into broad course marketplaces and platform-specific academies. Both teach *how the tool works*. Neither teaches an n8n freelancer how to price a job, win a client, and get placed—which is the entire wedge.

- **Udemy** — The scale player, over 50 million learners, with a wide catalog of pre-recorded no-code and n8n courses. Cheap and discoverable, but content is theory-heavy, tool-agnostic, frequently stale, and offers zero business coaching or job placement. Courses run **$10–$50, heavily discounted**.
- **Teachable** — A white-label course-hosting platform powering thousands of independent "edupreneur" micro-schools, including automation bootcamps. Flexible for instructors but fragmented in quality, with no native job board, community mentorship, or client simulation. Courses run **$50–$499, sometimes with subscriptions**.
- **Make (Integromat) Academy** — The official training arm of a rival automation SaaS. Strong, well-produced certification—but it exists to drive Make.com lock-in, ignores n8n entirely, and offers no business or job-placement layer. Free self-paced content up to roughly **$199 for certification**.
- **n8n docs, YouTube, and independent courses** — The current default: official documentation, community tutorials, and a handful of paid independent courses. Up to date and free-to-cheap, but fragmented, uneven, with no structured business curriculum or credential. Mostly **free, with paid courses in the $50–$199 range**.
- **Indirect: Zapier University, UiPath Academy, freelance consultancies** — Platform-tied academies (mostly free, vendor-locked) and one-on-one consultancies that do teach business skills but at **$500–$1,000+ per personalized engagement** with no community or scale.

**Your Opportunity**

Every incumbent either sells commoditized tool tutorials or is a vendor academy that will never build a tool-agnostic, business-first, job-connected product for n8n freelancers—it would dilute their positioning or cannibalize their model. Win on three things none of them will chase: (1) real client simulations plus pricing and proposal templates that turn builders into billers, (2) a certification that becomes the default trust badge in a credential-free niche, and (3) a two-sided job board whose liquidity compounds—more certified freelancers attract more client demand, which attracts more freelancers. Move first and the network effect locks the position before a fast-follower can catch up.

## Business Model

Recurring-membership SaaS with a low-friction free lead magnet, tiered subscriptions, and a job-placement take rate that turns the training audience into a marketplace. The path to roughly $1M ARR runs through about 1,200 blended paying members—for example 1,000 Membership subscribers plus 150 Agency Ready plus a steady trickle of Elite seats—well inside the reach of a 104,000-member subreddit and a 74,000-member Facebook group.

- **Free Starter Kit** ($0) — Downloadable workflow templates, a pricing cheat sheet, and quizzes; the lead-gen wedge that seeds the email list and community.
- **Membership** ($49/mo) — Weekly live coding sessions, the problem-solving community, and full Workflow Vault access—the continuity engine.
- **Agency Ready** ($149/mo) — Everything in Membership plus white-label workflows, client-acquisition training, proposal and pricing templates, and priority job-board visibility.
- **Elite** ($499) — One-to-one mentorship, priority job referrals, and a fast-track to certification for freelancers who want to compress time-to-income.
- **Certification** ($100–$300) — A standalone credential that becomes the trust signal on the job board and on client proposals.

**Unit Economics**

- **$60** — Target blended CAC (community-led, content-driven acquisition)
- **$85/mo** — Avg. revenue per paying member (blended across tiers)
- **~85%** — Gross margin (digital content plus community; near-zero marginal cost)
- **~$500** — LTV at a ~6-month blended membership life, before job-placement and certification revenue

## Recommended Tech Stack

The build is a membership site plus a community plus a two-sided job board with an AI workflow auto-grader—none of it novel infrastructure. The hard parts are gating content by tier, keeping video cheap at scale, and bootstrapping job-board liquidity, not the code.

- **Next.js 14 (App Router) on Vercel** — Server-rendered marketing and course pages for SEO against high-intent keywords like "n8n workflow" and "automation training"; Edge for auth and webhooks.
- **Convex** — Reactive backend for members, tiers, Vault workflows, cohort progress, job posts, and placements; real-time subscriptions power the live community and job feed without extra infra.
- **Stripe Billing** — Free, Membership, Agency Ready, and Elite plans plus one-time certification and placement fees; customer portal for self-serve upgrades and dunning.
- **Mux** — Video hosting and streaming for live sessions and the tutorial library; per-minute pricing keeps margin intact as the catalog grows.
- **Circle or Discord** — The gated community layer for weekly Q&amp;A, pricing clinics, and ambassador-led peer support, provisioned per tier via API.
- **Claude API (Anthropic)** — Powers the workflow auto-grader (parse a submitted n8n JSON export, score it against a rubric, return specific feedback) and a pricing assistant that drafts client proposals from a project brief.

## AI Prompts to Build This

Copy and paste these into Claude, Cursor, or your favorite AI tool.

**1. Project Setup**

```text
Build the weekend MVP of the "n8n Freelancer Academy": a Workflow Vault of ready-made n8n workflows and an AI grader that scores a member's own workflow against a real client brief. Membership level is set by hand this weekend.

Stack: Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind, Supabase (Postgres, Row Level Security, Auth with email magic link), the Anthropic API for the grading. Grading runs after the response returns, and the page updates when it finishes. Deploy on Vercel.

Tables (Row Level Security on; members read the vault by level and only their own submissions):
- profiles(user_id, display_name, tier, certified boolean)  tier is free or member, and you change it by hand
- workflows(id, title, category, description, json_export jsonb, tier_required)
- briefs(id, title, brief_markdown, scheduled_at)
- submissions(id, user_id, brief_id, workflow_json jsonb, score, feedback jsonb, facts jsonb, status, graded_at)  status is pending, graded or failed

Screens: /login, /vault, /vault/[id], /briefs/[id] (paste a workflow and see the grade).
Env vars (names only): NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY, SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (server only), ANTHROPIC_API_KEY.

Do not build: billing, plans or one-time purchases, video hosting, a community, a job board or placements, certification exams, live sessions.
Done when: npm run dev starts, you can sign in, and the four tables exist with Row Level Security on.
```

**2. Core Feature**

```text
Build the one feature that proves the Academy: a grade a member can act on, tied to nodes that really exist in their workflow.
1. The Vault lists workflows with their title, category and description. Copy JSON works only when the member's tier meets tier_required; otherwise the button explains the level needed.
2. On /briefs/[id], a member pastes an n8n workflow export. Reject text that is not valid JSON or has no nodes.
3. Clean the export before use: remove every credentials block and any value that looks like a key or token, and cut each node's parameters to 500 characters. Save the cleaned JSON.
4. Compute facts in code and save them to facts: node count, node types, whether an error trigger node exists, how many nodes set continue-on-fail or retry, and whether any node is disabled.
5. After responding, grade in the background: send the brief, the cleaned workflow and the facts to the Anthropic API with a strict JSON schema { score: number from 0 to 100, notes: [{ node_name, note }] } scored on structure, error handling and node efficiency, with exactly 3 notes.
6. Drop any note whose node_name is not a node in the workflow. If fewer than 3 notes remain, say so rather than inventing nodes. Save the score and notes, set status graded and let the page update on its own.
Rules: never keep the original, uncleaned JSON. Never block the page while grading.
Empty state: with no vault entries, show a message that the first workflows are coming.
Done when: a free member cannot copy a member-only workflow, a pasted export with a fake API key is saved without the key, a grade returns a score and notes that all name real nodes, and an invented node name is dropped.
```

**3. Landing Page**

```text
Build a one-page landing site for the n8n Freelancer Academy, training for people who sell n8n automations.
Hero: "Stop building $400 automations you should charge $2,500 for." Sub: "Real client briefs, a vault of workflows you can adapt and a grader that tells you what to fix." One button: Join the waitlist for the free Starter Kit.
Sections: the gap (free tutorials teach the tool and nobody teaches the business), how it works in four steps (take a brief, build, get graded, ship from the vault), a Vault preview with three sample entries, and an FAQ on what the grader checks, what is coming next (live sessions, a job board and certification) and how your workflow files are handled.
Waitlist: store the email in a waitlist table in Supabase. No other service.
Style: Geist, a clean off-white background, one mint accent, generous whitespace.
Done when: the page renders on a phone and a submitted email appears in the waitlist table.
```
