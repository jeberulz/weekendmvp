## The Problem

When an RFP reaches an SMB SaaS company, ownership often lands between the Account Executive and a Sales Engineer. The VP Sales wants the opportunity advanced, the Head of Solutions Engineering wants scarce technical capacity protected, and legal, security, product, finance, and customer teams may all hold pieces of the required evidence. No single person owns a complete, current answer set.

The first problem is qualification. Teams can begin writing before confirming mandatory fit, competitive position, buyer access, delivery capacity, or likely deal economics. That turns an attractive pipeline entry into a large hidden labor commitment. Storylane describes an unwinnable RFP as the most expensive one, while Arphie recommends deciding whether the opportunity deserves the team's time before drafting begins.

Once the bid proceeds, the document itself becomes a project. Sales Engineers read long files, transfer questions into spreadsheets, identify submission rules, and separate reusable questions from deal-specific requirements. Booma reports that sales and proposal teams spend an average of 25-30 hours per RFP. That work competes directly with discovery, demonstrations, proof-of-concept support, and technical follow-up on other deals.

Knowledge retrieval creates the next bottleneck. Product details may live in documentation, security answers in a separate questionnaire, commercial terms in email, and implementation evidence in old proposals. Copying a previous answer is fast but risky when the content is stale, unsupported, or written for another buyer. Generic AI drafting introduces another risk: fluent language can conceal an invented capability, an unsupported security claim, or an accidental roadmap commitment.

Review then expands across departments. Security specialists must inspect controls, legal must review exceptions, product leaders must confirm capability claims, and executives may need to approve pricing or contractual positions. Without a central workflow, contributors duplicate effort, lose context, and respond through disconnected threads. The response owner cannot easily see which requirements remain unanswered, which claims lack evidence, or which sections have final approval.

The buyer ultimately evaluates one submission, but the vendor produces it through a fragmented internal process. For SMB SaaS teams without dedicated proposal operations, the central pain is converting scattered organizational knowledge into a complete, defensible response before the deadline without exhausting the people needed to win every other active deal.

## The Solution

ProofBid is a deal-aware RFP response desk for SMB SaaS sales and solutions-engineering teams. Its initial wedge is the period between receiving a buyer questionnaire and routing the first informed draft for review. Instead of behaving like a general proposal writer, ProofBid turns each RFP into a controlled list of requirements, evidence, owners, risks, and approvals.

A Sales Engineer uploads the buyer's files and connects the active opportunity. ProofBid extracts questions, deadlines, mandatory attachments, formatting constraints, and evaluation signals. It then creates a bid brief covering mandatory fit, available evidence, known gaps, contributor capacity, and commercial context. Sales leadership retains the bid or no-bid decision, but the hidden response cost becomes visible before drafting consumes the team.

For approved bids, ProofBid retrieves answers from designated product documentation, security material, prior submissions, case studies, and implementation references. Every generated response carries its supporting source and confidence status. Unsupported claims, inconsistent source material, and possible roadmap promises are held back as exceptions rather than polished into authoritative prose.

The product's core workflow routes only those exceptions requiring human judgment. Security questions go to security owners, capability gaps to product or Sales Engineering, terms to legal, and commercial questions to sales leadership. Contributors can approve or edit answers without learning a large proposal-management system. The response owner sees coverage, risk, ownership, and approval status in one view.

ProofBid then exports approved content into the buyer's requested structure and provides a submission checklist. This narrow wedge delivers measurable value through reduced search, transfer, and coordination work while preserving human accountability for claims that can affect revenue, delivery obligations, or trust.

**How it works:**

1. **Deal Intake** — A Sales Engineer uploads Word, Excel, PDF, or questionnaire files and attaches account context from the active opportunity. The system identifies deadlines, submission rules, mandatory sections, evaluation criteria, and missing materials before anyone begins drafting.
2. **Bid Brief** — The assistant scores customer fit, mandatory capability coverage, available evidence, response capacity, and commercial context. It produces a reviewable bid or no-bid brief rather than making the decision automatically, preserving accountability with sales and technical leadership.
3. **Evidence Drafting** — Each requirement is matched against approved product documentation, security material, prior answers, and case studies. Drafts display their supporting sources and confidence status, while unsupported claims, conflicting evidence, and possible roadmap promises are explicitly flagged.
4. **Exception Review** — Routine supported answers move through quickly, while technical gaps, security exceptions, legal language, and commercial questions are routed to named owners. Reviewers approve, revise, or reject content, and the system preserves the decision trail for future responses.
5. **Submission Export** — Approved responses are returned to the buyer's requested structure, including original spreadsheets or document sections. The response owner receives a final checklist covering unanswered requirements, unapproved claims, attachment obligations, formatting constraints, and deadline details.

Do not build a full enterprise proposal-content suite, visual document designer, or autonomous submission agent before validating source-grounded drafting and exception routing.

## Business Model

Use transparent per-seat pricing with monthly document caps, while allowing unlimited reviewers so customers do not resist inviting legal, security, or product specialists. Essential Response should support teams with occasional RFPs and a small approved-source library. Deal Desk should increase document capacity, integrations, governance, and analytics. Meter imported RFP pages or completed questionnaires rather than individual AI prompts, because buyers can forecast that unit more easily. Offer annual billing without requiring enterprise-style implementation, and charge separately for unusually large document-processing overages.

## Recommended Tech Stack

Use a document-ingestion pipeline that preserves tables, question identifiers, workbook sheets, formatting constraints, and source locations across PDF, Word, and Excel. Retrieval should separate approved evidence from historical drafts and attach immutable source references to generated answers. A relational workflow store should track requirements, assignments, approvals, conflicts, and exports. Run model calls through structured schemas, maintain tenant-isolated vector indexes, encrypt uploaded deal documents, and log every source-to-answer relationship. CRM synchronization should import opportunity context without making the CRM the response system of record.

- **Next.js + TypeScript** — screens for Deal Intake, Bid Brief, Evidence Drafting, Exception Review, Submission Export
- **Postgres (Supabase or Neon)** — rfp_projects, source_documents, requirements, response_drafts, answer_evidence, review_assignments
- **Auth (Clerk or Supabase Auth)** — workspace seats and roles
- **Stripe Billing** — Essential Response / Deal Desk subscriptions
- **Vercel** — previews and production
