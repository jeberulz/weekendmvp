## The Problem

SMB SaaS Sales Engineers and Solutions Engineers often become the human routing layer for complex buyer questionnaires. An RFP arrives as a spreadsheet or document containing product, security, privacy, legal, implementation, and commercial questions. The assigned owner must search prior submissions, internal policies, help-center pages, product notes, and messages from subject experts. Even when an answer exists, the owner still has to determine whether it is current, approved, relevant to the prospect, and safe to reuse.

The drafting burden is only part of the problem. Account Executives want speed because the document may block evaluation or procurement. Security and Legal reviewers want precision because a casual statement can become a customer expectation. Revenue leaders want predictable deal support without turning every questionnaire into an internal project. These incentives collide in inboxes and spreadsheets where ownership, evidence, and approval are difficult to see.

Community behavior suggests that practitioners already perceive value in applying language models to stored answers. A user reported successful results after manually providing a security answer library and buyer question to GPT-4 ["I’ve already tried this - it worked great! Copy/paste my library of security questions and answers into GPT-4, copy paste the customer question, answers were as accurate as a human doing it!"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36488436). That experiment demonstrates the appeal of fast drafting, but the described copy-and-paste workflow does not itself establish source freshness, access control, approval history, or a durable audit trail.

Established automation can also leave workflow friction unresolved. A Loopio user described stronger matching and completion for consistently structured security questions while also finding the interface difficult to navigate ["Another tool that I've been happy with is Loopio. They do have the "Magic" capability that tries to automate answers. Given the consistent structure of security questions, they had a higher match / completion rate, but their UX was a little difficult to navigate. Again, software only solution, but something that might be interesting for comparisons."](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26513040). This matters for SMB teams because the person responding may not be a dedicated proposal manager. A feature-rich repository becomes counterproductive if occasional contributors cannot quickly understand what requires attention.

The core pain is therefore not simply writing prose. It is producing a defensible answer from fragmented company knowledge while preserving speed. Teams need to distinguish supported claims from guesses, route exceptions to the right owner, and return the completed file in the buyer's requested format. Without that system, experienced Solutions Engineers spend valuable deal time on retrieval and coordination, while less experienced contributors risk reusing stale language or accepting fluent output without evidence.

## The Solution

ProofDraft would be a grounded RFP response assistant designed around the working habits of SMB SaaS sales and solutions teams. Its wedge would be narrow: ingest a buyer questionnaire, retrieve approved company knowledge, draft concise responses with source citations, and place uncertain items into a human review queue. The product would not attempt to replace the Sales Engineer or become a general proposal-design suite.

A team would begin by importing policies, product documentation, help-center content, approved prior answers, and reviewer guidance. ProofDraft would break these materials into traceable passages while preserving source, owner, approval state, and freshness. When a new spreadsheet or document arrives, the system would identify questions, classify topics, locate supporting passages, and prepare answers constrained by those passages.

The primary interface would emphasize exceptions rather than presenting a blank editor. Reviewers would see unsupported claims, conflicting sources, stale material, missing ownership, and buyer questions that require clarification. High-confidence drafts would remain editable, but their evidence would stay visible beside the response. Approval actions would capture who validated the answer and which source supported it.

After review, ProofDraft would export answers into the original document structure and add approved language back to the reusable library. Usage caps would align with active RFPs and generated answers, giving SMB teams predictable packages while limiting model and document-processing costs. The initial promise would be straightforward: help a small revenue team answer buyer requests faster without sacrificing provenance, reviewer control, or the final judgment of accountable employees.

The workflow below is ProofDraft's proposed first version: a plan to build, not a tested product.

**How it works:**

1. **Build the evidence library** — ProofDraft would ingest security policies, product documentation, approved prior responses, legal guidance, and help-center material. It would preserve source location, ownership, freshness, and approval state so retrieval favors current, authorized knowledge rather than whichever response happened to appear in a recent document.
2. **Parse the buyer request** — A user would upload a spreadsheet or document, and ProofDraft would separate instructions, questions, requested formats, dependencies, and unanswered fields. It would classify each item by product, security, legal, privacy, implementation, or commercial topic and route uncertain items to an appropriate reviewer.
3. **Draft with traceable support** — For each question, ProofDraft would retrieve relevant passages and compose a concise answer constrained by the source material. Every material claim would carry an inline citation, while unsupported requests would be marked for clarification instead of receiving a plausible but ungrounded response.
4. **Review and deliver** — Sales Engineers and subject experts would work through an exception-focused queue showing low-confidence drafts, stale sources, conflicting passages, and requested edits. Approved answers would update the reusable library, and the completed response would export into the buyer's original structure without erasing provenance.

What not to build yet (scope advice for ProofDraft's first version, not research):

Do not build a broad proposal designer, contract-negotiation suite, or autonomous buyer-portal agent before grounded drafting and review are trusted.

## Recommended Tech Stack

A suggested stack for ProofDraft (a recommendation, not research):

Use a web application with Next.js and TypeScript, backed by PostgreSQL for workflow records and pgvector for passage retrieval. Store original files and normalized exports in encrypted object storage. A document pipeline should parse DOCX, PDF, XLSX, and CSV while preserving sheet, cell, page, and section coordinates for citations. Route model calls through a provider abstraction with prompt versioning, redaction, usage metering, and tenant isolation. Add background workers for ingestion, embedding, export, and freshness checks. Authorization should support reviewer roles, source-level restrictions, audit logs, and eventual SOC 2 readiness.

- **Next.js + TypeScript** — screens for Build the evidence library, Parse the buyer request, Draft with traceable support, Review and deliver
- **Postgres (Supabase or Neon)** — source\_documents, source\_passages, rfp\_projects, rfp\_questions, answer\_drafts, citation\_links
- **Auth (Clerk or Supabase Auth)** — workspace seats and roles
- **Stripe Billing** — Proof Solo / Deal Desk / Solutions Pod subscriptions
- **Vercel** — previews and production
